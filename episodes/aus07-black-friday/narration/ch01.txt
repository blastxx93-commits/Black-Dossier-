First, let's be clear about what we know, and what we don't.

As I record this, most car brands haven't announced their 2026 Black Friday deals yet. They usually land in the second half of November. So I'm not going to invent offers that don't exist. Instead, I'll show you what happened last year, what's being offered right now, and what to watch for when the deals drop.

Let's start with 2025, because it tells us what to expect.

Suzuki ran a short Black Friday sale, from the 21st to the 23rd of November, with one thousand dollars cashback on the Vitara, S-Cross and Ignis, while stocks lasted. Omoda Jaecoo offered two thousand dollars cashback plus three years of free scheduled servicing across the Jaecoo J7, the J8 and the Omoda 9, with the J7 starting from $32,990 drive-away. Geely offered gift cards, free charging cables and cheap finance on the EX5 and the Starray EM-i. Chery offered two thousand dollars off selected models. And according to Savvy, Isuzu advertised drive-away discounts of between five and eight thousand dollars on the D-Max ute and the MU-X.

And the year before, in 2024, the deals were even bigger on some slow-selling models. Skoda took ten thousand dollars off the last 135 Superb Sportline sedans and wagons. Mazda cut nearly ten thousand dollars from selected CX-60 variants. BYD took three thousand dollars off most of its range and threw in a wall charger, although installation wasn't included. And Suzuki offered ten per cent off its range, including the Jimny – but only on cars already in stock, and with delivery required by the 30th of November.

Notice something? Most of those deals came from Chinese brands, or from brands fighting to hold on to market share. And most of them weren't simple price cuts. They were cashback, gift cards, free servicing or finance deals. That matters, and I'll explain why later.
