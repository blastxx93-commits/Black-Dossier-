Now let's talk about something every new car owner in Australia should understand in 2026: recalls. Because this year, there have been a lot of them.

In September, BYD recalled 32,009 Shark 6 utes over possible abnormal wear in the spare wheel assembly. Owners were told they could remove the spare wheel if they wanted to keep driving while they waited for the fix.

In October, Ford recalled 1,085 Rangers and Everests from the 2024 to 2026 model years, because the 10.1-inch centre screen can go blank – which means the reversing camera image may not appear. That's a real safety risk for anyone standing behind the car. Ford also recalled the big F-150 pickup over a fire risk the same month. CarExpert notes that Ford issues more recalls in Australia than any other car maker.

Suzuki's new Fronx small SUV was halted from sale after a rear seatbelt failure in ANCAP crash testing. And Deepal had a stop-sale order on its E07.

Here's what you need to know. A recall is the manufacturer admitting a fault and fixing it for free – and that's the system working. You should always get recall work done. But a recall doesn't cancel your consumer rights. If the fix can't be done within a reasonable time, or the same problem keeps coming back after the repair, you may be entitled to more than just a recall repair. In some cases, that can mean a refund or a replacement.

You can check whether your car is affected on the government's Product Safety Australia recall website, using your car's make and model, or by asking your dealer to run your VIN.
