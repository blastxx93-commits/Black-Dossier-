So what should you do if you need a ute? First, this is a buyer's market. Dealers are carrying stock, brands are running drive-away deals, and loyalty bonuses are back. Don't pay sticker price.

Second, check the run-out stock. When a new model year arrives – like the MY26.5 Ranger – the outgoing stock is often where the best deals are.

Third, think about resale. Heavy discounting on a new model can drag down used values later. If you're buying a ute that's struggling to sell new, expect it to be worth less when you trade it in.

And finally, deals change every month. Everything we've covered here was correct when it was announced, but always confirm the current drive-away price with the dealer before you sign anything.

A few more tips before you hit the yards. Ask for the drive-away price in writing, so there are no surprises with on-road costs. If you have an ABN, ask about business pricing – a lot of the sharpest ute deals in 2026 were for ABN holders. Look at the build date on the compliance plate, because a ute that's been sitting in the yard for months is exactly the kind of stock dealers want to move. And if you're thinking about a plug-in hybrid like the Shark 6, work out how much of your driving could be done on electric power. If you charge at home and do short trips, the running costs can be very different from a diesel. If you tow heavy loads long distances every week, the maths might change completely.

Most importantly, don't let a big discount talk you into the wrong ute. The cheapest ute is the one that actually fits how you live and work.
