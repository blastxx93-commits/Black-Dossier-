### ch00_hook
For fifteen years, Australia has been ute country. Tradies, farmers, families, weekend campers – everyone wanted a dual-cab. But right now, something has quietly broken. In July 2026, sales of 4x4 utes fell more than fifteen per cent in a single year. Australia's best-selling ute lost its crown. One brand-new ute sold barely two hundred units in a month. And one dealer boss says you can drive to the ports and see cars sitting everywhere.

So which utes are feeling the squeeze? Who's cutting prices to move them? And if you're shopping for a ute in 2026, is this the best time in years to buy – or a trap? Stick around, because number one on this list is a ute nobody expected to see here.

### ch01
Let's start with the numbers, because they tell the whole story. According to the industry's official VFACTS data, the 4x4 pick-up segment dropped from 17,351 sales in July 2025 to 14,705 in July 2026. That's a fall of 15.2 per cent. And it's not a one-month blip. Across the first eight months of 2026, 4x4 ute sales were down 13.6 per cent.

Now here's the strange part. June 2026 was the biggest single month for new car sales in Australian history. So Aussies are still buying cars. They're just buying fewer utes. Diesel sales overall were down about thirteen per cent through the first part of the year as buyers moved into hybrids, plug-in hybrids and electric SUVs.

At the same time, choice has exploded. There are now around twenty-five different utes on sale in Australia. Back in January 2025, Toyota Australia's then-boss Sean Hanley floated the idea that Australia had hit "peak ute" – that more utes wouldn't mean more ute buyers. Eighteen months later, it looks like he had a point.

And when supply goes up while demand goes down, there's only one place for the pressure to land: the dealer's yard. Mark Beitz, managing director of the Bartons Motor Group, told CarsGuide there was excess inventory everywhere, with cars piled up at the ports. Add the New Vehicle Efficiency Standard, which pushes brands to sell lower-emission vehicles, and diesel utes have become harder to make money on.

So let's count down the ten utes feeling it the most.

### ch02
Starting at number ten: the LDV T60. LDV has always been the budget option, and it's leaning into that harder than ever. In late 2025, LDV dropped the T60 Max Pro 4x4 automatic to $36,990 drive-away for ABN holders. That's a $2,000 cut, and at the time it made the T60 the cheapest dual-cab ute with a tub in the country.

The T60 also picked up safety gear that was missing before, including autonomous emergency braking. But here's the catch. When the cheapest dual-cab in Australia still needs a discount to stand out, that tells you how crowded the bottom of the market has become. Chinese rivals are now fighting over the same budget buyer, and LDV's price advantage is shrinking every month.

### ch03
Number nine is the Kia Tasman. This was a big deal when it launched. Kia's first ute, developed with Australia firmly in mind, and backed by one of the strongest brands in the country.

But the sales haven't followed the hype. In May 2026, the Tasman 4x4 managed 382 sales. In July, 355. For comparison, the Toyota HiLux 4x4 did 4,119 that same July. That's more than eleven HiLuxes for every Tasman.

To be fair, it's early days, and Kia overall is doing well – it was Australia's third best-selling brand in July. But in a slumping segment, a new ute doesn't get years to find its feet. If those numbers don't climb, expect to see sharper deals on Tasman stock in your local Kia dealer.

### ch04
At number eight, the Volkswagen Amarok. Underneath, the current Amarok is a Ford Ranger – built alongside it in South Africa. Same bones, German badge, higher price.

And that's the problem. In May 2026, the Amarok sold just 271 units. Volkswagen's answer was to go upmarket with the new Amarok W600, developed with Walkinshaw – a name Aussie car fans will know from the Holden Special Vehicles days. But the W600 is priced about $1,300 above the Ford Ranger Raptor, which is the most famous performance ute in the country.

It's a bold bet. Volkswagen's product manager told the press that innovation in the ute segment has never been greater. Maybe. But it's hard to win buyers when your cheaper cousin is sitting in the Ford showroom across the road.

### ch05
Number seven: the Mitsubishi Triton. Now, the Triton isn't in trouble the way some others are. After a quiet 2025, it bounced back in early 2026, posting 1,747 4x4 sales in February and briefly fighting for third place.

But here's why it's on this list. Mitsubishi's biggest new rival might be… Mitsubishi. The brand-new Nissan Navara is essentially a rebadged Triton, tuned locally by Premcar. So in showrooms across Australia, the same basic ute is now competing against itself under two badges. By July, Triton 4x4 sales had slipped to 1,081. When two brands split one ute's buyers in a shrinking market, somebody ends up discounting.

### ch06
At number six, the Isuzu D-Max. The D-Max is the steady achiever – tough, simple, reliable, and loved in the bush. In February it held onto third spot among utes, and in July it was still the third best-selling 4x4 ute with 1,292 sales.

So why is it here? Because the pressure is coming from underneath. The BYD Shark 6 has been trading blows with the D-Max for that third spot, and more electrified utes are on the way. GAC has confirmed its ute will be electrified only, and Chery's diesel-electric Stockman is due in late 2026. The D-Max is a diesel ute in a market that is slowly turning away from diesel. It's not falling yet – but it's standing on shifting ground.

### ch08
Number five is the GWM Cannon Alpha. GWM has been one of the big success stories of the Chinese wave, and the Cannon range has been a real player in the ute segment.

But look at the price tags. As part of its eighteenth-birthday celebrations, GWM cut $4,000 off the Cannon Alpha Ultra diesel, bringing it to $54,990 drive-away until the end of October 2026. A $4,000 cut on a large dual-cab is serious money. It shows that even the brands winning market share have to fight hard to move utes right now – because every buyer they win, they're winning off a competitor who is also discounting.

### ch09
At number four, the Nissan Navara. This one hurts, because the Navara has been an Aussie favourite for decades.

Nissan as a brand had a rough 2025. Sales fell 21.6 per cent, and the old Navara fell 28 per cent. The new-generation Navara – that Triton twin we mentioned – was supposed to turn it around. But in May 2026 it managed 217 sales. In July, 212.

So Nissan reached for the price lever. Until the end of August, the base Navara was offered at $49,990 drive-away, down from $53,348 before on-road costs. The top Pro-4X dropped to $63,990 drive-away from $68,418 plus on-roads. Existing Nissan owners were offered a $2,000 loyalty bonus on top. Those deals have now ended, but they tell you exactly how hard Nissan is pushing. A brand-new model being discounted this heavily, this early, is a warning sign.

### ch10
Number three: the BYD Shark 6. This is the ute that shook up the whole segment. A plug-in hybrid, more than 300 kilowatts, priced right against the Ranger and HiLux. It became Australia's best-selling plug-in hybrid and, at its peak, the fourth most popular ute in the country.

But 2026 has been bumpier. In March, Shark sales dropped 53.2 per cent to 1,314 deliveries. BYD ran a drive-away deal at $57,900 for the Shark 6 Premium in February and March, including rego, CTP and stamp duty. And then came a recall affecting 32,009 Shark 6 utes over possible abnormal wear in the spare wheel assembly. BYD told owners they could remove the spare wheel if they wanted to keep driving while waiting for the fix.

To be clear, the Shark is still a big seller – it was the third best-selling 4x4 pick-up across the first half of the year. But it shows that even the disruptor isn't immune. Everyone in this segment is fighting for a smaller pie.

### ch11
Number two is the Toyota HiLux. Yes, the HiLux. For years it was untouchable.

In early 2026, Toyota as a whole was down around 25 per cent, which Toyota blamed on supply problems during model changeovers. The HiLux was down 19.1 per cent year-on-year in May. Through the first part of the year, about 2,600 fewer HiLuxes found homes compared to 2025.

There's also a product question. The latest HiLux is a heavy update rather than an all-new ute, and rivals have been quick to point that out. Toyota also launched a HiLux electric ute, but with a fairly short range it's a niche product – only fourteen were delivered in May.

Now, here's the twist. In July 2026, the HiLux actually beat the Ranger, 4,119 to 3,827, likely because new stock finally arrived. So the HiLux isn't dying. But Toyota fighting stock shortages while the whole segment shrinks means every month is now a battle.

### ch12
And number one. The ute nobody expected on a list like this: the Ford Ranger. Australia's best-selling vehicle. The Aussie-engineered hero.

So why is it here? Because even the king is discounting. Ford has been offering $3,000 off selected Ranger 4x4 variants – including the plug-in hybrid – plus, in some periods, five free general services. That's ahead of the updated MY26.5 range arriving. Ford sold about 1,600 fewer Rangers in the first part of 2026 than the year before, mostly in the expensive 4x4 grades. And in July, it lost the monthly top spot among 4x4 utes to the HiLux.

Think about what that means. If the most popular ute in Australia needs a few thousand dollars off and free servicing to keep moving, then every ute below it is under even more pressure. That's the real story of 2026.

### ch13
So what should you do if you need a ute? First, this is a buyer's market. Dealers are carrying stock, brands are running drive-away deals, and loyalty bonuses are back. Don't pay sticker price.

Second, check the run-out stock. When a new model year arrives – like the MY26.5 Ranger – the outgoing stock is often where the best deals are.

Third, think about resale. Heavy discounting on a new model can drag down used values later. If you're buying a ute that's struggling to sell new, expect it to be worth less when you trade it in.

And finally, deals change every month. Everything we've covered here was correct when it was announced, but always confirm the current drive-away price with the dealer before you sign anything.
