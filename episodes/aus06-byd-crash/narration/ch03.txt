Now the car arrives at the repairer. And this is where things get expensive, because modern cars hide a lot behind the bumper.

A rear bumper on a new BYD isn't just a piece of plastic. It can carry parking sensors, a reversing camera and radar units for the safety systems. After a hit, those sensors often need to be replaced or at least recalibrated, and calibration needs special equipment and time. A bump that would have cost a few hundred dollars on a twenty-year-old car can turn into a bill of several thousand.

Then there's the battery. Even if it looks fine, the repairer and the insurer need to know whether the impact has damaged the pack or its protective casing. On some models, that means removing the battery for inspection. And if the pack is damaged, the cost can be huge. In a report published in April 2026, actuarial firm Taylor Fry said the battery can make up around thirty to fifty per cent of an electric car's value. Replace the battery, and you may be looking at most of the cost of the car.

That's why some EVs with relatively modest damage end up written off. Not because the car is destroyed, but because the risk and cost of repairing the battery is too high compared to what the car is worth.

Industry insiders have described cases where electric cars were written off because the battery casing was scratched, even though the cells inside were probably fine – simply because nobody had the diagnostic information to prove the pack was safe. And when an insurer writes off an EV, it doesn't get much back. In 2024, an assessing industry manager said the salvage value of a written-off electric car was only around eight to ten per cent of its replacement value, because there was so little demand for used EV batteries and parts. Every one of those write-offs is a cost that ends up spread across everyone's premiums.

To be clear, not every insurer sees it the same way. Allianz, for example, has said that cosmetic EV repairs generally take about as long as petrol car repairs, and that it had no data showing EVs are written off more often. The problem is concentrated in the serious crashes – the ones that reach the battery and the high-voltage system.
