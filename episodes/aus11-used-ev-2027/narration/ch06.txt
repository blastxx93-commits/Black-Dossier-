Now here's some good news for anyone worried about buying a used EV: the batteries.

The biggest fear people have with a second-hand electric car is the battery. Will it be worn out? Will it cost a fortune to replace? Pickles looked at battery health data from more than 2,500 battery electric vehicles. On average, the state of health was ninety-six per cent. For vehicles over five years old, it was ninety-three per cent. And even for vehicles that had done more than 120,000 kilometres, it was eighty-nine per cent.

That's a lot better than many people expect. It doesn't mean every battery is perfect – charging habits, climate and the specific model all matter. But it does suggest most used EVs on the market still have most of their battery capacity.

Buyers have noticed, too. The average used EV sold by Pickles in the September quarter was about 3.1 years old, with roughly 44,000 kilometres – about fifty per cent more kilometres than two years earlier. And they're still selling. That suggests buyers are becoming more comfortable with higher-kilometre EVs, which is exactly what the 2027 lease wave will bring.

And there's another surprising number. Pickles found that statutory write-off EVs sold for sixteen per cent more than comparable petrol and diesel vehicles, and repairable write-offs sold for seventy-one per cent more. Damaged EVs are in demand, mostly for their parts and batteries.
