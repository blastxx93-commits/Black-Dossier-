First, let's understand where this wave is coming from.

In July 2022, the federal government made eligible electric cars exempt from fringe benefits tax when they're provided through an employer – including through a novated lease. That's what's called the Electric Car Discount. And it changed the market. Suddenly, a lot of workers could afford a new EV, paying for it out of their pre-tax salary.

Novated leases typically run for around three to five years. So the first big group of those cars, leased from mid-2022 onwards, is now reaching the end of its term. When a lease ends, a lot of those cars get handed back, sold at auction, or traded in. And that means more used EVs, all arriving at roughly the same time.

NRMA noted in July 2026 that the Electric Car Discount had just reached its fourth anniversary. Pickles told the NRMA it expected more salary-packaged EVs to be remarketed over the following twelve months. And by October 2026, Pickles reported ex-novated volumes in the September quarter were 157 per cent higher than in the first quarter of the year.

To give you a sense of scale: more than 100,000 battery electric cars were sold new in Australia in 2025 alone, according to CarsGuide – about 8.3 per cent of the market. By September 2026, EVs were making up more than twenty-four per cent of new car sales in a single month. Every one of those cars will eventually become a used car. The used EV market is only going to get bigger from here.

And there's another twist. As we covered in our last video on 2027 EV changes, the Electric Car Discount itself is being wound back. From the 1st of April 2027, the full exemption only applies to new EVs costing $75,000 or less. That could change how many people choose to take out a new lease on an expensive EV – and how many decide to buy used instead.
