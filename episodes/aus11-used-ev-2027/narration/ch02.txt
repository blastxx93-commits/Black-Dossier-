Now, here's what makes this story more interesting than a simple "prices are crashing" headline. 2026 has actually been a strong year for used EVs.

Why? Fuel. When petrol and diesel prices spiked earlier this year, a lot of Australians started looking at electric cars – including second-hand ones. According to the Australian Automotive Dealer Association and AutoGrab, used EV sales in the first half of 2026 rose about fifty-four per cent compared with the year before – to more than twenty-five thousand cars. At the same time, total used car sales fell 6.6 per cent.

And used EVs started selling faster. The NRMA reported that the average time to sell a used EV fell from more than sixty days in January to under forty days in June.

Pickles had its biggest quarter on record for used EVs in the September quarter of 2026, with more than 440 sold, and more than two hundred in September alone. Over the twelve months to September, it sold more than 1,580 used EVs – more than double the year before.

Which brands are people buying second-hand? According to the NRMA's report on AADA and AutoGrab data, Tesla was by far the biggest, with 7,402 used sales in the first half of 2026 – up forty-eight per cent. MG was second by volume, with 2,582. Kia had 1,927 – more than double the year before. BYD had 1,767, up almost 175 per cent. And Polestar and Volkswagen both grew from small bases, with Volkswagen up more than 1,300 per cent.

Interest exploded too. Pickles said its EV pages drew more than 20,000 visitors in the June quarter of 2026 – up from about 2,000 a year earlier. And carsales reported that EV searches jumped almost seventy-seven per cent in a single week in March 2026, when fuel prices were soaring.

And it's not just city buyers. Pickles said regional buyers took thirty-two per cent of its used EV purchases in the 2026 financial year, up from twenty-seven per cent. In Queensland, almost half of its used EV sales went to regional buyers.
