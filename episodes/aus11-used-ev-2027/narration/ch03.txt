So what happened to prices?

Pickles uses an index from Datium Insights to track used car values. Its Used EV Index rose from 104 in February 2026 to 122 in May – that was the peak of the fuel crisis. Then it eased back to 107 by September. In other words, used EV prices jumped during the fuel spike, and then started coming back down.

You can see the same pattern in Pickles' auction prices. The NRMA reported the average used EV sale price at Pickles was about $35,500 in January, rose to about $41,500 in April, then fell back to $35,500 in May and about $31,500 in June. And the average odometer reading went up – from about 29,000 kilometres earlier in the year to about 51,000 kilometres by May and June. More older, higher-kilometre EVs are coming through.

And remember, the long-term picture for EV depreciation has been tough. A 2025 analysis by Pickles and Datium Insights found that after two years, a Tesla Model 3 kept about fifty-four per cent of its value, a Model Y about fifty-eight per cent, an MG4 about fifty per cent, and a BYD Atto 3 about fifty-four per cent. Compare that with a Toyota RAV4 at about eighty-eight per cent, a LandCruiser at about eighty-eight, and a HiLux at about eighty-five.

There's another way to measure this: how much of their value cars hold compared with similar vehicles, and how often sellers have to cut the price. The AADA and AutoGrab track this for cars aged one to five years. In May 2026, they put the average residual value for hybrids at about ninety-four per cent, plug-in hybrids at about eighty-four per cent, and electric vehicles at about seventy-five per cent. So EVs were still holding value less well than hybrids.

But fewer EVs needed a discount. The share of used EVs that had to be discounted fell from about forty-six per cent in January to forty-four per cent in May. For petrol cars, it was about fifty-one per cent, and for diesel almost fifty-two per cent. So in 2026, used EVs actually needed fewer price cuts than petrol and diesel cars.

So the question for 2027 is simple. When thousands of ex-lease EVs arrive, will demand keep up? Or will prices fall again?
