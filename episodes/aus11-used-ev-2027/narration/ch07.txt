So what does 2027 look like? Here's my honest read, based on the data – not a prediction anyone can promise.

On one side, supply is going up. More ex-novated EVs are coming back. More brands are selling EVs. More EVs are reaching three, four and five years old. Pickles' own manager says the current volumes are "really just the beginning."

On the other side, demand is also going up. More Australians are considering EVs, regional buyers are getting involved, and dealers are getting more confident. Pickles' Brendon Green said dealers increasing their participation "tells us they're becoming more confident." The AADA's James Voortman said: "As more EVs enter the used market and consumers become increasingly comfortable with the technology, we would expect the used EV market to continue to mature."

And then there are the wildcards for 2027. Fuel prices – if they spike again, used EV demand will jump. New EV prices – if they keep falling, used values will follow. The fringe benefits tax change in April 2027. And the planned NSW road user charge for EVs from July 2027.

And don't forget the condition of the cars themselves. The first wave of ex-lease EVs will mostly be three to five years old, with one previous owner, and often with full service histories – because a novated lease usually includes scheduled servicing. That's exactly the kind of car many used buyers are looking for.

So rather than a single crash, the more likely picture is a market that keeps getting bigger and more competitive – with older, more expensive and higher-kilometre EVs under the most pressure.
