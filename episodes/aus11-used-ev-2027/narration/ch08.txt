So what should you do if you own an EV and you're thinking of selling?

First, know your numbers. Check comparable listings for your exact model, year and kilometres. Get a valuation or an instant offer. That's your starting point.

Second, think about timing. Carsales suggested selling sooner may suit owners nearing the end of their battery warranty, owners whose car has been overtaken by a cheaper or updated model, or people who bought early and still have equity. Holding may suit people who owe more than the car is worth, are near the end of a lease or finance term, or are happy with the car.

Also, think about where you sell. With regional buyers now taking almost a third of Pickles' used EV sales, the buyer for your car might not be in your suburb. Listing nationally, or selling through a channel that reaches regional buyers, could get you a better price.

Third, prove the battery. A battery health report can make a big difference to buyers. Keep your service history and any software updates documented.

And fourth, if your car is coming off a novated lease, compare your residual payment with the car's real market value. If the car is worth more than the residual, you may be able to keep or sell it for a profit. If it's worth less, talk to your lease provider about your options before the end date.
