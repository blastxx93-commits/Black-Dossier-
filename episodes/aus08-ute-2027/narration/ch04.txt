There's another change on the way that could affect some of Australia's toughest vehicles.

According to CarsGuide, a draft change could bring vehicles with a gross vehicle mass between 3,500 and 3,855 kilograms into the NVES from the 1st of July 2027. Right now, heavier vehicles sit outside the scheme.

Why does that matter? Because some popular heavy-duty vehicles sit right in that band. CarsGuide named the LandCruiser 70 Series, with a GVM of 3,510 kilograms, and the Toyota Tundra, at 3,536 kilograms. Both would be pulled into the emissions scheme if that draft change goes ahead.

And this fight over utes isn't new. Back in 2024, before the NVES became law, the Housing Industry Association warned that "applying stringent efficiency standards with harsh penalties will substantially drive up the price" of utes and vans. At the time, the government's consultation paper claimed compliance would cost manufacturers about $190 per ute or van. Two years later, we're about to find out who was closer to the truth.

It's still a draft. But if you're a farmer, a miner or a serious tourer looking at a LandCruiser 70, it's something to keep an eye on in 2027.
