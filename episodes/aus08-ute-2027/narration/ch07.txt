So what are the alternatives? If diesel utes get more expensive, what else is there?

In May 2026, CarExpert listed every electrified ute on sale in Australia. The cheapest was the GWM Cannon Alpha plug-in hybrid, from around $57,490 drive-away in New South Wales. Then the Ford Ranger plug-in hybrid from around $59,000. The KGM Musso EV from around $60,000. The BYD Shark 6 from around $62,660. The Deepal E07 electric from around $70,137. And the Toyota HiLux electric from around $82,752.

The fuel crisis has changed the maths for a lot of buyers. In May 2026, diesel reached as high as $3.50 a litre in some parts of Australia, according to CarExpert. Filling an 80-litre Ranger tank could cost up to $280. Ford cut the price of the Ranger plug-in hybrid by up to ten thousand dollars, and the Wildtrak hybrid dropped to $70,000 drive-away – and dealer stock was soon "all but exhausted", according to Ford. As a Ford spokesperson put it: "Australian buyers are doing the maths."

And a price war has started in plug-in hybrid utes. In May 2026, CarsGuide reported that Ford had cut older Ranger plug-in hybrids to a recommended $62,000 drive-away, down from a $71,990 sticker price. The new JAC Hunter plug-in hybrid was targeting less than fifty thousand dollars drive-away. And Toyota's own 48-volt mild hybrid diesel HiLux starts from $57,990 before on-road costs.

More are on the way. Chery has a diesel-electric ute due before the end of 2026, with a petrol-electric version expected in 2027. GWM plans a plug-in hybrid version of its smaller Cannon ute. And Nissan's next-generation Navara is set to use Mitsubishi's plug-in hybrid technology. By 2027, ute buyers will have more electrified choices than ever before.

And Toyota itself sells an electric HiLux, from $74,990 to $82,990 before on-road costs. For comparison, the diesel HiLux 4x4 range runs from $45,990 to $71,990 before on-road costs. So right now, the electric version is still a lot more expensive.

That's the squeeze in a nutshell. Diesel utes face rising penalties. Electrified utes are getting cheaper, but most are still more expensive up front, and they don't suit everyone – especially for heavy towing and long outback trips.
