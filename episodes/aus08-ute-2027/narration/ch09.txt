So, what does all this mean if you're planning to buy a ute?

First, nobody has announced a 2027 price rise yet. Toyota said it "cannot rule out" increases. That's a warning, not a price list. Don't panic-buy because of a headline.

Second, if you were already planning to buy a diesel ute in the next few months, it's worth knowing that 2026 runout stock and year-end deals are on the table right now. If the price is right and the ute suits you, there's no reason to wait for things to get more expensive.

Third, work out your real costs. With diesel prices where they've been in 2026, a plug-in hybrid ute can save a lot on fuel if you do lots of short trips and charge at home. But if you tow heavy loads long distances, check the real-world range and towing figures very carefully before switching.

And if you're thinking about a plug-in hybrid ute through a novated lease, remember that plug-in hybrids lost their fringe benefits tax exemption on the 1st of April 2025. Only fully electric vehicles still qualify – and as we'll see in the next video, even that changes in 2027.

If towing is your priority, compare the braked towing capacity, not just the price. Some electrified utes now match diesel on paper – the MG U9 electric ute, for example, claims 3,500 kilograms of braked towing. But range drops a lot when you tow, so look for real-world tests before you commit.

Fourth, think about resale. If emissions rules keep tightening, the value of different types of utes could change over the next few years. Nobody knows exactly how. But it's worth asking yourself what your ute will be worth when you sell it in five years.

And fifth, watch the NVES review and the federal politics. If the targets are softened, or the scheme is repealed, the pressure on ute prices could ease. If they stay, 2027 is when the squeeze really starts.
