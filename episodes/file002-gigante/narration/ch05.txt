CHAPTER FIVE. The boss nobody could name.

By 1980, Gigante had risen to consigliere, the adviser to the boss. A year later, when the old leader stepped down for health reasons, Gigante took control of the Genovese organization.

But he did something no one outside the family expected. He did not take the title in public.

Instead, the world was shown a front man: Anthony "Fat Tony" Salerno, a cigar-chewing veteran who sat in a social club in East Harlem and looked every bit like a Mafia boss. Investigators photographed him, followed him, and planted listening devices in his club, the Palma Boys Social Club. Hour after hour, the tapes recorded Fat Tony talking business, cursing, giving orders. To the FBI, it sounded like the voice of the boss himself.

It was exactly what Gigante wanted.

In 1986, in one of the biggest prosecutions in American history, Fat Tony Salerno was convicted as its supposed leader, and later sentenced to one hundred years in prison.

The real chief kept shuffling down Sullivan Street in his robe, untouched.

Only later did a Genovese mobster named Vincent Cafaro turn informant and tell the FBI the truth. Salerno had been a front all along. The shuffling madman was the one in charge.

By then, Gigante had a new enemy.

On December 16th, 1985, Gambino boss Paul Castellano stepped out of his car outside Sparks Steak House in Manhattan, during the evening rush, a few days before Christmas. Gunmen in matching trench coats and fur hats were waiting. Castellano and his driver were shot dead on the sidewalk. The man behind the murder was an ambitious captain named John Gotti, who then took over the Gambino organization.

Killing a boss without the approval of the Commission broke the oldest rule in the Mafia. Gotti had done it anyway, and then he had done something even worse in Gigante's eyes: he had become a celebrity. Expensive suits, cameras everywhere, a smile for every photographer. The Dapper Don was everything the Chin was not. And Gigante wanted Gotti dead for it.

On April 13th, 1986, a car bomb exploded in Brooklyn. It killed Frank DeCicco, Gotti's new underboss. According to later testimony, the bomb had been ordered by Gigante together with the leader of the Lucchese clan, and it was meant for Gotti too.

But Gotti had canceled at the last minute.

He survived. And before long, it would be Gotti's own right-hand man who walked into a courtroom and told the world what the Chin truly was.
