DESCRIÇÃO (copie tudo entre as linhas)
--------------------------------------------------------------
May 2nd, 1957. In the lobby of the Majestic on Central Park West, a former boxer shouts "This one's for you, Frank" and fires at Frank Costello, the Prime Minister of the Underworld. Thirty years later, the same man is shuffling through Greenwich Village in a bathrobe and slippers, talking to parking meters, while the FBI calls him the most powerful Mafia boss in America.

Was Vincent "The Chin" Gigante really insane? How did a man in pajamas run the Genovese crime family? Why were his soldiers forbidden to say his name? And what did he finally admit in a Brooklyn courtroom in 2003?

In this documentary you'll discover:
🔺 The night Frank Costello was shot in the lobby of the Majestic
🔺 How a 21-win boxer became Vito Genovese's protégé
🔺 The Kefauver hearings and Costello's famous hands
🔺 Why Costello refused to identify the man who shot him
🔺 Gigante and Genovese: convicted together, sharing a prison cell
🔺 The bathrobe, the psychiatrists and the "Odd Father" act
🔺 The chin-touch rule inside the Genovese family
🔺 Fat Tony Salerno: the front boss who took the fall
🔺 The Castellano murder and the car bomb meant for John Gotti
🔺 Sammy "The Bull" Gravano's testimony
🔺 The 1997 trial and the 2003 confession

Vincent Gigante (1928–2005), known as "The Chin" and "The Oddfather", was a former professional boxer from Greenwich Village who rose under Vito Genovese. He was acquitted of the 1957 attempted murder of Frank Costello, served time with Genovese for heroin trafficking, and from 1969 onward claimed to suffer from paranoid schizophrenia, walking Sullivan Street in a bathrobe while investigators say he ran the Genovese crime family from the Triangle Civic Improvement Association.

After a seven-year fight over his mental competency, he was convicted of racketeering in 1997, with testimony from Sammy "The Bull" Gravano and Alphonse D'Arco. In 2003 he pleaded guilty to obstruction of justice and admitted the insanity had been an act. He died in federal custody in Springfield, Missouri, in 2005.
