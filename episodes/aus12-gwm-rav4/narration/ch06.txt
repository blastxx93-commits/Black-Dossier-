First, towing. Australians love to tow. CarExpert lists the new H7's braked towing capacity at 1,500 kilograms, for both the hybrid and the plug-in hybrid. That's fine for a small trailer or a light camper. But if you're planning to tow a large caravan or a boat, check the exact towing figures for every car you're considering before you buy – and remember that plug-in hybrids use more fuel and electricity when towing.

Second, warranty. GWM offers a seven-year, unlimited-kilometre warranty on its vehicles, according to its Australian website. And its hybrid and plug-in hybrid batteries are covered for eight years with unlimited kilometres. One detail in the fine print: for plug-in hybrids and EVs, GWM says a battery state of health of seventy per cent or more isn't considered a warranty defect.

Toyota's base warranty is five years with unlimited kilometres. CarsGuide reported that Toyota extends engine and driveline cover to up to seven years, and hybrid battery cover to up to ten years, as long as the car is serviced and maintained according to the warranty and service book. So on paper, the warranties are closer than they first look – but the conditions are different, and it's worth reading them both.
