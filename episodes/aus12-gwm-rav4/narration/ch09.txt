So, which one should you buy? Here's how I'd decide.

Buy the GWM Haval H7 plug-in hybrid if price, power and electric range matter most to you. If you do lots of short trips, can charge at home, want all-wheel drive and a long warranty, and plan to keep the car for a long time – so resale value matters less – the H7 offers a lot of car for the money.

Buy the Toyota RAV4 plug-in hybrid if resale value, brand reputation and dealer network matter most. Toyota's history of holding value and its huge service network, especially in regional Australia, are real advantages. And if you can wait until early 2027, the cheaper GXL plug-in hybrid could narrow the price gap.

And think about how you'll actually use the battery. A plug-in hybrid only saves you real money if you plug it in. If you can't charge at home, or you mostly do long highway trips, a regular hybrid – like the H7 hybrid or the RAV4 hybrid – may make more sense, and it's cheaper to buy. With petrol and diesel prices as volatile as they've been in 2026 – diesel hit as high as $3.50 a litre in some parts of Australia in May, according to CarExpert – the right choice depends on your driving, not the brochure.

On the test drive, pay attention to a few things. How does the car drive when the battery is flat and the petrol engine is doing the work? Is the engine noisy? How easy are the touchscreen and the driver assistance systems to live with? And how much boot space is left once the charging cable is in there? These are the things that make a car pleasant or annoying after the first week.

And whichever you choose, do these four things. Test drive both. Compare drive-away prices, not sticker prices. Check towing capacity if you tow. And look at the real running costs: fuel, electricity, servicing and insurance.
