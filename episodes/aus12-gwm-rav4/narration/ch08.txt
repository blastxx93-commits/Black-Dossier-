Before we get to Toyota's counter-attack, there's one more thing worth considering: the dealer and service network. Toyota has one of the largest dealer networks in Australia, including in regional towns. That matters for servicing, warranty repairs and crash repairs. As we saw in our video on BYD after the crash, a great car can still be a frustrating ownership experience if parts take weeks or the nearest approved repairer is hours away. GWM has been in Australia for eighteen years and has a substantial network, but before you buy any car, check where your nearest service centre and approved repairer actually are.

And here's the twist: Toyota isn't standing still.

In the first three months of 2027, Toyota is adding a cheaper RAV4 GXL plug-in hybrid. Toyota hasn't announced the price yet. CarExpert estimated it could be around $53,990 before on-road costs, based on the gap between the hybrid and plug-in hybrid versions of the RAV4. That's an estimate, not an official figure. It's expected to use the same 201-kilowatt plug-in hybrid system with a claimed 121 kilometres of electric range, and to become the most affordable RAV4 plug-in hybrid. CarExpert also said it's expected to undercut the Mitsubishi Outlander plug-in hybrid, which starts at $58,990 before on-road costs.

Why is Toyota moving so fast? Partly because of the government's emissions rules. Under the New Vehicle Efficiency Standard, the target for passenger cars drops from 117 grams of carbon dioxide per kilometre in 2026 to 92 grams in 2027. Plug-in hybrids help car makers hit those targets. Toyota is already earning credits thanks to its hybrids – but it needs them, because its utes and big four-wheel drives are well above the targets. Every RAV4 plug-in hybrid it sells helps balance the books.

So in 2027, the gap between the RAV4 PHEV and the H7 PHEV could shrink – although if that estimate is right, the RAV4 would still be more expensive once on-road costs are added.

GWM is moving too. The seven-seat H7, due in 2027, gets a longer 2,850-millimetre wheelbase and stretches to 4,912 millimetres long. That puts it into the territory of bigger family SUVs – a part of the market where Toyota doesn't sell a RAV4 at all.

And GWM won't be the only one fighting for RAV4 buyers. The plug-in hybrid SUV market is packed with Chinese rivals – from BYD, Chery, Geely and Omoda Jaecoo – many of them with big batteries, lots of equipment and sharp drive-away prices. In September 2026, BYD was Australia's second best-selling brand. The pressure on Toyota is real.
