Let's put them side by side.

Plug-in hybrid versus plug-in hybrid. The H7 Lux PHEV: $49,990 drive-away, 320 kilowatts, all-wheel drive, 137 kilometres of claimed electric range. The RAV4 XSE PHEV front-wheel drive: $58,840 before on-road costs, 201 kilowatts, 121 kilometres of claimed electric range.

On those numbers, the GWM is around nine thousand dollars cheaper before you even add the RAV4's on-road costs. It's much more powerful. And it claims more electric range. The H7's electric range figure is on the Chinese WLTC cycle, though, so it's not a perfect like-for-like comparison with every rival. Real-world range will depend on how and where you drive.

On features, the H7 Lux also comes with a lot as standard for the price, including that 15.6-inch touchscreen and a head-up display. When you compare the two in a showroom, make a list of what you actually need – and check which features are standard on the exact grade you're looking at, not just the top model in the brochure.

And for families who mostly do short trips, a claimed 137 kilometres of electric range is a big deal. If you charge at home every night, many commutes and school runs could be done without burning any petrol at all.

Let's do a quick illustration – and I want to be clear, these are example numbers, not official figures. Say you drive forty kilometres a day, about 14,600 kilometres a year. A petrol SUV using seven litres per hundred kilometres, at two dollars a litre, would cost around $2,044 a year in fuel. If you could do most of that driving on electricity charged at home, and your plug-in hybrid used around twenty kilowatt-hours per hundred kilometres at thirty cents per kilowatt-hour, that's around $876 a year. Your own numbers will depend on your electricity plan, your driving and the car. But it shows why electric range matters so much for daily drivers.
