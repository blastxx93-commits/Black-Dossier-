First, a bit of context about who GWM is. Great Wall Motors – GWM – has been selling cars in Australia for eighteen years, and in October 2026 it was celebrating that anniversary with price cuts across its range. Its Haval Jolion small SUV has become one of the best-selling cars in Australia in 2026. And like most Chinese brands, it's been growing fast while some long-established brands go backwards.

You can see that shift in the official sales numbers. In September 2026, according to VFACTS, Geely sales were up 870 per cent on the same month a year earlier, Omoda Jaecoo was up 344 per cent, Leapmotor 106 per cent and BYD 61 per cent. Toyota, by comparison, was down 3.5 per cent. Toyota is still the biggest brand in the country by a long way – but the pressure is coming from every direction.

First, let's meet the challenger.

The new GWM Haval H7 is a boxy, five-seat mid-size SUV. It's actually the second H7 GWM has sold here in a short time. CarExpert reported that the outgoing model was replaced after "a little over a year" on sale – and to the end of July 2026, GWM had delivered only 636 of the old H7s, compared with 8,759 of its Haval H6. So the new one has a lot to prove.

It's also bigger than you might expect. CarExpert lists the five-seater at 4,680 millimetres long – 4,800 including the spare wheel carrier on the tailgate – and 1,950 millimetres wide, with a 2,738-millimetre wheelbase. That boxy shape is a big part of its appeal. It looks more like a rugged off-roader than a typical family SUV.

It comes in two powertrains, both with all-wheel drive as standard.

The hybrid uses a 1.5-litre turbo petrol engine with electric motors, for 182 kilowatts and 670 newton-metres, with claimed fuel use of 7.0 litres per hundred kilometres.

The plug-in hybrid uses the same 1.5-litre turbo petrol engine, but with a much bigger 33.7-kilowatt-hour battery. Combined output is a huge 320 kilowatts and 670 newton-metres. GWM claims 0 to 100 kilometres an hour in 4.9 seconds, an electric-only range of 137 kilometres, and a combined range of 844 kilometres on the Chinese WLTC test.

And GWM says interest has been strong. It reported around 1,500 expressions of interest after the H7's debut at the Everything Electric show in September. GWM Australia's head of marketing, Steve Maciver, said: "We have high expectations for the all-new Haval H7."
