So what should you do right now?

First, don't panic. As of September 2026, no road-user charge was being collected anywhere in Australia, and the federal government has not legislated one.

Second, if you're buying an electric car or a plug-in hybrid in New South Wales, factor in a possible charge from July 2027. At around three cents per kilometre, budget roughly four to five hundred dollars a year for typical driving. It still usually works out cheaper than fuel – but it's not nothing.

Third, keep good records of your odometer readings and servicing. Every proposal so far relies on distance, and accurate records protect you if you're ever charged.

And fourth, have your say. Federal inquiries and state reviews take public submissions. Motoring clubs, dealer groups, EV groups and car companies are all lobbying hard. Ordinary drivers should too.

Here's the timeline to watch. NSW's charge is due from the 1st of July 2027, unless it's delayed, changed or challenged in court. The motoring clubs want a federal charge legislated in the 2027 budget. And the next federal election will decide which party gets to write the rules.

So, could every Aussie driver end up paying a new road tax? It's being seriously proposed – by Tesla, by electric vehicle groups, by car dealers and by motoring clubs. The federal government says it's focused on EVs, and it isn't promising a date. But the old system is running out of road, and change is coming.
