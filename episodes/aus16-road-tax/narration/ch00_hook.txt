Fifty-three point seven cents. That's how much tax you pay on every single litre of petrol or diesel in Australia right now. Fill up a big ute, and you've handed the government around forty dollars before you've even left the servo.

Now imagine paying that – and a new tax for every kilometre you drive on top of it.

That's not a conspiracy theory. In October 2026, Tesla – the world's best-known electric car company – told a federal parliamentary inquiry that petrol, diesel and hybrid drivers should pay a per-kilometre road charge, while keeping fuel excise. The Australian Electric Vehicle Association has proposed something similar. Polestar's Australian boss says a road-user charge is "inevitable". And New South Wales already has a law for a per-kilometre charge on electric cars, due to start in July 2027.

So in this video, I'll explain exactly what's being proposed, who wants it, what it could cost you – whether you drive a Corolla, a LandCruiser or a Tesla – and what you can actually do about it. Because the way Australians pay for roads is about to change, and it's going to affect every driver in the country.
