Tesla isn't the only one pushing for a charge on everyone.

On the 17th of September 2026, the Australian Electric Vehicle Association – a volunteer-run, not-for-profit group – released its own proposal. Electric cars would pay a road-user charge. Petrol and diesel vehicles would pay the same charge, plus the existing fuel excise, which the association calls a "highly efficient pollution tax".

Its version is based on weight and distance. The rate would phase in over ten years to three cents per tonne-kilometre. The association's own example: a 2.2-tonne diesel ute or electric SUV driven 13,000 kilometres a year would pay $858 annually. Odometer readings would be taken every six or twelve months.

AEVA policy convenor Dr Chris Jones said that for vehicles of equal weight and distance, "it's only fair that they pay the same road user charge. But if one of those vehicles burns petrol or diesel, they should pay for the cost of that pollution too." He added: "We're not after a free ride, but we are asking for policy which encourages EV adoption."

Polestar agrees with the principle. In October 2026, Polestar Australia's managing director, Scott Maynard, told CarExpert he sees "the inevitable introduction of a road-user charge", and that it should apply to all motorists, based on weight. He pointed out: "We don't have a congestion tax in Australia."

And it's not just electric car companies. The Australian Automotive Dealer Association, led by James Voortman, has called for a national road-user charge for all vehicle types, as part of a full review of vehicle taxes. The Australian Automobile Association has called for a distance-based charge, developed with the states and modelled on the NSW plan.
