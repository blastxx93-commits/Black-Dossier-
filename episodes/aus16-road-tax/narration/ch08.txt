So what could this actually cost you? Let me be clear: these are illustrations based on the published proposals, not confirmed prices. None of them is law at the federal level yet.

Say you drive an electric car 15,000 kilometres a year in New South Wales. At the legislated NSW rate of 3.095 cents, that's about $464 a year.

Now say the charge was set at the level that would roughly match what a petrol car pays in excise. Secondary reporting of a Parliamentary Budget Office costing puts that at about 5.3 cents per kilometre. Over 15,000 kilometres, that's around $795 a year.

Under the electric vehicle association's weight-based plan, that 2.2-tonne ute or SUV doing 13,000 kilometres would pay $858 a year. If it's a diesel ute, that comes on top of fuel excise. A ute using around nine litres per hundred kilometres would burn about 1,170 litres over that distance – roughly $628 in excise at today's rate. Together, that's nearly fifteen hundred dollars a year in road taxes for one vehicle.

And under Tesla's proposal, newly registered petrol, diesel and hybrid cars would also pay both – the exact rate would depend on what governments decided the real cost of roads is.

That's why this matters to every driver, not just EV owners. The difference between the proposals is the difference between a few hundred dollars a year and well over a thousand.
