Before we finish, let's look at October – because some of the monthly numbers are even more shocking than the countdown.

According to CarExpert's sales database, 88 models recorded zero sales in October. Now, to be fair, many of those are cars that have been axed or are between generations. But some of the low numbers are on cars that are very much still on sale.

The Audi Q4 e-tron: two sales in October, down 96 per cent. Audi has now cut the price of the updated model by up to eighteen thousand dollars, to get it under the luxury car tax threshold – but deliveries of that model don't start until 2027.

The BMW iX electric SUV: four sales, down 86 per cent. The Hyundai Ioniq 9 seven-seat electric SUV: four. The Suzuki S-Cross: two, down almost 90 per cent. The Renault Kangoo van: two, down almost 97. The Fiat 500: three. The Peugeot 308: three. And the Alfa Romeo Tonale: one single car, down 80 per cent.

And here's one that looks like a disaster, but isn't quite what it seems. The Toyota Fortuner sold just eight cars in October, down more than 97 per cent. But that's because Toyota has axed it in Australia, with no replacement. Dealers are simply selling the last ones. That's why it isn't in my top ten.
