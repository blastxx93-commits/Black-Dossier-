So who's paying the price? Every Chinese sale has to come from somewhere – because the market isn't growing.

Total new-vehicle sales for the first nine months of 2026 were 914,312, almost exactly the same as the year before – just 127 cars fewer. That means this is a zero-sum game. When BYD gains forty thousand sales, someone else loses forty thousand.

Here's who's losing. Toyota is down 15.9 per cent year to date, to 152,944, with its market share falling from 19.9 per cent to 16.7. Mazda is down 16.2 per cent. Mitsubishi is down 30.2. Subaru is down 29.3. Nissan is down 33.3 per cent.

Every one of those is a Japanese brand. According to analysis by John Cadogan at Auto Expert, Japanese-built vehicle sales in the first eight months of 2026 fell around 22 per cent – from almost 220,000 to around 170,000. Over the same period, Chinese-built sales rose about 71 per cent, from roughly 122,000 to almost 209,000.

It's not just the Japanese, either. Korean brands Hyundai and Kia were both down around twenty per cent in September. Volkswagen was down almost 37 per cent. The established brands that dominated Australian driveways for decades are all going backwards at the same time.

To put the scale of this into perspective: Toyota alone has sold almost 29,000 fewer vehicles this year than at the same point in 2025. Mazda is down more than eleven thousand. Mitsubishi, Subaru and Nissan have lost tens of thousands between them. Meanwhile BYD has added almost thirty-nine thousand, Geely twenty thousand and Chery fourteen thousand. The money hasn't left the market. It's simply moved to different showrooms.
