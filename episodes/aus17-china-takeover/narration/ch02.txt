Now let's look at the brands doing the damage, using official VFACTS figures for the first nine months of 2026.

BYD is the headline. Year to date, it has sold 76,614 vehicles, up 102 per cent. It's now Australia's second-best-selling brand, behind only Toyota. And in June, it came within 243 cars of Toyota in a single month – 18,881 to 19,124. A few years ago, most Australians had never heard of BYD.

Then there's Chery, up 58.2 per cent to 38,022. Its Tiggo 4 small SUV was the sixth-best-selling vehicle in the whole country in September.

Geely is the most extraordinary story of all. Its sales are up 639 per cent this year, to 23,632. In September alone, Geely was up 870 per cent on the same month in 2025. Its EX5 electric SUV sold 2,008 cars that month – up 620 per cent – and finished eighth on the national sales chart.

Geely's premium brand, Zeekr, went from just 730 sales at this point last year to 12,120. Omoda Jaecoo was up almost twelve hundred per cent in the first half. GWM, the longest-running Chinese brand here, was up about twenty per cent, and its Haval Jolion was seventh in September. Even MG, owned by China's SAIC, kept growing.

And the Chinese brands are moving upmarket fast. BYD's luxury brand, Denza, had passed 1,600 sales since arriving, according to CarsGuide. And in late October 2026, CarExpert reported that the Denza B5 – a big plug-in hybrid four-wheel drive aimed squarely at the Toyota LandCruiser Prado – had been cut to under seventy-five thousand dollars drive-away. A Chinese luxury 4x4 taking on the Prado would have sounded ridiculous five years ago. Now it's on sale at your local dealer.

Tony Weber, chief executive of the Federal Chamber of Automotive Industries, summed it up in July: "The Australian automotive market has shifted on its axis during the first months of 2026." And BYD's chief operating officer, Stephen Collins, put it more simply: "We asked our customers to back us in, and they did, and we delivered."
