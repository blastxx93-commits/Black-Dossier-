Number three: the Tesla Cybertruck.

This one is a disaster of a different kind – because the car never even arrived.

In November 2019, Tesla started taking one-hundred-and-fifty-dollar deposits from Australians for the Cybertruck. Thousands of people put their names down. Then they waited. And waited. The reservation portal closed in May 2022 and never reopened.

Then, on the 23rd of October 2026, Tesla emailed deposit holders with this: "After careful consideration, plans to produce Cybertruck for Australia and New Zealand have been discontinued." Almost seven years after the first deposits were taken.

The reason is simple. The Cybertruck is built only in left-hand drive, and it isn't sold in any right-hand-drive country. Tesla is refunding deposits, and offering a free year of its Full Self-Driving software to buyers who order a Model 3 or Model Y instead – CarExpert valued that at $1,788.

To be fair, a hundred and fifty dollars won't break anyone. But seven years of waiting for a truck that was never coming? That's a disaster for Tesla's reputation with some of its most loyal fans.
