Number four: Jeep.

The original off-road brand. In September 2026, the whole of Jeep sold just sixteen cars in Australia, across every model.

According to CarExpert, Jeep's sales in the first half of 2026 fell 66 per cent to just 365 vehicles. The Compass was down 84 per cent and is no longer sold here. The Grand Cherokee was down almost 97 per cent, with stock nearly run out. The Gladiator ute was down 56 per cent. And the little Avenger, CarExpert simply described as a flop.

In October, the Jeep Compass sold one car, and the Grand Cherokee and Avenger sold none.

The problem is price. The cheapest four-door Wrangler went from around thirty-seven thousand dollars in 2014 to almost seventy-six thousand before on-road costs. At that money, Australians are buying a Prado, an Everest, or a Chinese four-by-four with far more equipment.

Jeep is now offering four thousand dollars towards your deposit on the Wrangler Rubicon. And reports say Jeep may use Chinese partner Dongfeng for future models. For a brand built on American heritage, that tells you how bad things have become.
