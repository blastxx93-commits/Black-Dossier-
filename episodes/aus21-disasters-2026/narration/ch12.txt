Before the verdict, a few dishonourable mentions – disasters that only just missed the top ten.

The Kia EV9. A seven-seat electric SUV from $97,000 before on-road costs. In the first nine months of 2026, it found 120 buyers, down almost 50 per cent. Half its buyers, gone in a year.

The Audi Q4 e-tron. Just two sales in October, down 96 per cent. Audi has now cut the price of the updated model by up to eighteen thousand dollars – but deliveries of that car don't start until 2027.

Polestar. In a year when electric car sales in Australia more than doubled, CarExpert reported that Polestar's sales were down ten per cent year to date. The Polestar 4 was down almost a quarter in September. Polestar's local boss, Scott Maynard, defended the result, saying the Polestar 4 is "absolutely flying" – but the overall numbers went backwards in the middle of an EV boom.

The Toyota Fortuner. Eight sales in October, down more than 97 per cent. Toyota has axed it in Australia, with no replacement. Even the biggest brand in the country has its failures.

Mitsubishi. Sales down 50 per cent in October alone. For a brand that was a top-five player not long ago, that's a brutal month.

And three brands that recorded exactly zero new-car sales in October: Jaguar, Lotus and McLaren. To be fair, Jaguar has stopped selling new cars while it reinvents itself as an electric luxury brand, so its zero is planned. But a month with zero sales is still a striking number for brands that so many people know.
