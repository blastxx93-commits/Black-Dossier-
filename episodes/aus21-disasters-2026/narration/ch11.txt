And number one: the Kia Tasman.

Kia's first-ever ute. It was developed with Australia in mind, and before it launched, Kia was talking big. According to GoAuto, the target was twenty thousand sales, and seven to ten per cent of the ute market, in its first year.

The Tasman arrived in July 2025. And according to the official VFACTS figures, reported by GoAuto, Kia had sold 7,898 Tasmans from launch to the end of September 2026. That's fifteen months, and not even half the first-year target.

Kia hasn't hidden from it. Kia Australia's CEO, Damien Meredith, said early in the year: "We're not making or looking for excuses." And in May, Kia's global planning boss, Spencer Cho, told CarExpert: "Clearly we are not satisfied yet." He said "all the options are on the table" to fix it, including more power or a hybrid.

The discounts have been huge – up to thirteen thousand dollars off the X-Pro earlier in the year, according to CarExpert, and in November the 2025 Tasman SX+ was advertised at $54,990 drive-away, down from $62,390 before on-road costs. And in Korea, local media reported that production was being cut because of weak demand.

Why didn't it work? Critics have pointed to the styling and the lack of a hybrid. Kia has pointed to the aggressive pricing of rival ute makers, at a time when the Ford Ranger, the Toyota HiLux and BYD's Shark 6 are fighting hard for every buyer. Whatever the reason, the Tasman is more than twelve thousand sales short of its first-year plan – even with three extra months. That's why it's number one.
