So what do these ten disasters have in common?

Most of them made the same mistake: they arrived too expensive, or too late, in a market that changed faster than anyone expected. Chinese brands moved in with cheaper hybrids and electric cars, and buyers stopped paying a premium for a badge.

And for you, that matters in three ways.

First, if you're buying, disasters mean deals. When a car isn't selling, the dealer has the most room to move. Ask for a written drive-away price, ask how long the car has been in stock, and ask about demonstrators.

Second, think about resale. A car that's hard to sell new is usually hard to sell used. Plan to keep it longer, and make sure the discount covers the extra depreciation.

Third, if you already own one of these cars, don't panic. Your warranty and consumer rights don't change because sales are low. Check for recalls, keep your service history up to date, and know where your nearest service centre will be.

So that's the list: the Mazda CX-70, the Mustang Mach-E, the Honda Accord, Maserati, Peugeot, Nissan, Jeep, the Tesla Cybertruck, the Hyundai Palisade – and at number one, the Kia Tasman.
