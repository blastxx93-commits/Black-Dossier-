Number nine: the Ford Mustang Mach-E.

This one should have been a hit. It has one of the most famous badges in the world, and it's an electric SUV in a year when electric car sales in Australia exploded.

Instead, the Mach-E sold 206 cars in the first nine months of 2026, down more than 34 per cent. In all of 2025, it found fewer than five hundred buyers.

Ford tried to fix it with price cuts. EV Central reported drive-away offers of sixty thousand dollars for the Select, seventy-five thousand for the Premium and eighty-nine thousand for the GT – cuts of up to around nine and a half thousand dollars on earlier list prices. EV Central described it as Ford being "desperate to shift" stock.

To understand how big this disaster is, look at the competition. In October alone, the Tesla Model Y sold 3,105 cars. The Mach-E sold 206 in nine months. In the United States, Ford sold more than fifty thousand Mach-Es in 2025. In Australia, against a wall of cheaper Chinese electric SUVs, it simply disappeared.
