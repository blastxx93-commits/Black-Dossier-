First, a quick look at the scoreboard, because it explains why this list looks the way it does.

Australians bought 107,493 new vehicles in October 2026, a record for the month. Year to date, the market has already passed one million sales.

But where did the money go? Electric car sales in October were up 237 per cent on October last year, and electric cars took more than 23 per cent of the market. BYD sold more than ten thousand cars in a single month and is now firmly the second biggest brand in Australia, behind only Toyota. Cars built in China were up 104 per cent.

In the first nine months of the year, Geely was up 639 per cent. Omoda Jaecoo was up 621 per cent. And Zeekr was up a staggering 1,560 per cent.

Meanwhile, plenty of familiar names went backwards – Mitsubishi, Subaru, Nissan, Volkswagen, Hyundai and Mazda all had double-digit falls.

So what makes a winner in 2026? Price, technology, and timing. The winners on this list are mostly cheaper, newer, and more efficient than the cars they're replacing in buyers' driveways. But not all of them are Chinese – and two of the biggest comebacks are from Toyota.

A quick note on the numbers. I'm using official VFACTS figures through CarExpert, CarsGuide and GoAuto. Tesla doesn't report to VFACTS, so its figures come from the Electric Vehicle Council. Let's go.
