Before the wrap-up, some honourable mentions – cars that just missed the top ten, but had a brilliant 2026.

The Ford Ranger. It's not growing – sales were down about ten per cent in the first nine months – but with 37,858 sales, it was still the best-selling vehicle in Australia for the year to September. In a year of upheaval, staying number one is a success of its own.

The new-generation Toyota RAV4. It had a slow start while the old model ran out, but by September it was back to second in the country, with 4,624 sales, up 81 per cent. In July, around four in ten RAV4s sold were plug-in hybrids – a big change for Australia's favourite SUV.

The BYD Shark 6. Australia's first plug-in hybrid ute to really break through. In October, it sold 1,843, up 72 per cent, and finished ninth in the country – ahead of the Isuzu D-Max and the Mitsubishi Triton for the month.

The Chery Tiggo 7. In the first half of 2026, its sales were up 173 per cent, to 5,589. Chery's bigger SUV is following the Tiggo 4's success.

The Hyundai Kona. In a terrible year for Hyundai, with the brand down almost 29 per cent in October, the Kona went up 9 per cent in the first nine months, with 18,533 sales. It's proof that the right car can still win, even when the brand is struggling.

And GAC – a Chinese brand that only arrived recently and had already sold 3,175 cars by the end of September. Just one more sign of how fast new brands are growing here.
