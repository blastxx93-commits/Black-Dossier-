October 1964. Park Avenue, New York. Almost midnight.

A light rain is falling. A taxi pulls up at the corner of Thirty-Sixth Street, and two men in overcoats step out onto the wet sidewalk.

One of them is a lawyer. The other is his client, a silver-haired man of fifty-nine, who in a few hours is due to appear before a federal grand jury.

He never makes it to the door.

Two strangers come out of the dark. One grabs him by the arm.

"Come on, Joe. My boss wants to see you."

The lawyer shouts. A gunshot cracks into the pavement at his feet, and he runs for cover. By the time he looks up, the car is gone. And so is Joe Bonanno.

Boss of one of the five families of New York. A member of the Mafia's ruling Commission for more than thirty years.

For nineteen months, nobody knows if he is alive. Not the FBI. Not his own son. Not the men who run the Mafia.

And when he finally walks back into the light, the story he tells may be the greatest lie in the history of Cosa Nostra.
