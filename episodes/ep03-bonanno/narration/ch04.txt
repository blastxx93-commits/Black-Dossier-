CHAPTER FOUR. A king without a crown.

In the Mafia, ignoring the Commission is not a small thing. It is a declaration.

Bonanno's answer was to leave the country.

In 1964 he went to Montreal, where his organization had deep roots, and he applied to become a Canadian citizen. The Canadian authorities had other plans. He was arrested, held for almost three months in Montreal's Bordeaux Prison, and finally forced to leave.

He came home to a different world.

In his absence, the Commission had taken the only step it had left. Joe Bonanno was no longer boss of his own men. In his place, the bosses recognized one of his captains, a quiet, older man named Gaspar DiGregorio.

DiGregorio had been best man at Bonanno's wedding. Now he sat in his chair.

At the same time, the federal government wanted to speak with him too. A grand jury in Manhattan was investigating organized crime, and Bonanno was ordered to testify.

So by October 1964, Joe Bonanno was trapped between two courts. The Mafia had stripped him of his crown. The United States wanted him under oath.

On the night of October twentieth, he went out to dinner with his lawyers, to prepare for his testimony.

The next morning, he was supposed to be in court.
