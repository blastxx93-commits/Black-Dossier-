CHAPTER ONE. The youngest boss in America.

Giuseppe Bonanno was born in January 1905, in Castellammare del Golfo, a fishing town on the northwest coast of Sicily. In that town, the old families did not call themselves criminals. They called themselves men of honor.

His father was one of them.

When Giuseppe was a small child, the Bonannos crossed the ocean to Brooklyn, to Williamsburg. A few years later they sailed home again. By the time he was ten, his father was dead. A few years after that, his mother was gone too.

In 1924, nineteen years old and alone, he decided to try America a second time. He had no visa. So he slipped aboard a Cuban fishing boat bound for Florida, in the company of a friend from home named Peter Magaddino.

Remember that name. Forty years later, the Magaddinos will be at the center of this story.

Bonanno landed in Tampa, made his way north, and found work in the business that ran New York in the age of Prohibition: illegal liquor. His protector was a fellow Castellammarese, an educated, ambitious boss named Salvatore Maranzano.

In 1930, a war broke out between Maranzano and Joe Masseria, the strongest boss in the city. It became known as the Castellammarese War. Bonanno fought on Maranzano's side, and Maranzano won.

His victory lasted five months.

In September 1931, Maranzano was murdered in his own office. The killers were sent by a younger generation led by Lucky Luciano.

Luciano did not crown himself king. Instead, he built a board of directors: a Commission, where the bosses of the major families would settle disputes together, instead of with machine guns.

Maranzano's old crew needed a new leader. They chose Bonanno.

He was twenty-six years old. The youngest boss in America, with a seat on the ruling council of the biggest criminal organization the country had ever seen.
