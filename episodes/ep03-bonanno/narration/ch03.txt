CHAPTER THREE. Nowhere to run.

Ignoring the Commission was a declaration of war. Bonanno answered it by leaving the country.

In 1964, he went to Montreal and tried to become a Canadian citizen. It did not work. Canadian police arrested him and locked him in Bordeaux Prison for almost three months. Then they threw him out.

He came back to New York, and to a new reality.

The Commission had removed him. Officially, Joe Bonanno was no longer in command of his own men. In his place, the bosses recognized one of his captains, a quiet, older man named Gaspar DiGregorio.

The detail that made it hurt: DiGregorio had been best man at Bonanno's wedding.

And the Mafia was not his only problem. A federal grand jury in Manhattan wanted him under oath, to answer questions about organized crime.

So by October 1964, Bonanno was trapped between two courts. One could send him to prison. The other could send him to the cemetery.

On the evening of October twentieth, he sat down to dinner with his lawyers to prepare his testimony.

He had one night left.
