CHAPTER THREE. The plot.

To this day, what Bonanno was planning in 1963 is told in two very different ways.

According to the Commission, and to most historians, it was a plan to kill.

The targets were the bosses who stood in his way: Carlo Gambino, Tommy Lucchese, Stefano Magaddino, and Frank DeSimone, the boss of Los Angeles. With them gone, Bonanno would be the undisputed master of the American underworld.

He could not do it alone. He needed a partner.

He found one in Joseph Magliocco, the heavy, sickly man who had taken over the Profaci crew after Joe Profaci's death. Magliocco had his own grudges against the Commission. He agreed.

The contract was handed to one of Magliocco's most trusted captains, a young, ambitious mobster named Joe Colombo.

It was the biggest mistake of their lives.

Colombo looked at the plan, and he did the math. Killing the bosses of the Commission was a gamble. Betraying the plot to them was a promotion.

He went straight to the targets, and told them everything.

The Commission summoned Magliocco and Bonanno to explain themselves.

Magliocco came. He was ill, frightened, and out of options. He confessed. The bosses decided that the real mind behind the plot was not his, and they spared his life. He paid a fine of fifty thousand dollars, and he was ordered to step down.

His crew went to the man who had betrayed him: Joe Colombo. The Profaci family would one day carry his name.

Magliocco did not enjoy his retirement. Within months, his heart gave out.

Bonanno did not come at all.
