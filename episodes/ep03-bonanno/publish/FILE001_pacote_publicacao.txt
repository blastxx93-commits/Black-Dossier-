October 1964. Park Avenue, New York. Near midnight, in the rain, two gunmen grab Mafia boss Joe Bonanno in front of his lawyer and push him into a car. A shot hits the sidewalk. And one of the most powerful men in the American Mafia disappears for 19 months.

Who took him? Where was he? Why did the FBI, his own soldiers and the Mafia's ruling Commission all lose track of him? And why, years later, was an FBI agent behind a wave of bombs aimed at the Mafia in Arizona?

In this documentary you'll discover:
🔺 How a 26-year-old Sicilian became the youngest Mafia boss in America
🔺 The Commission: the ruling council created by Lucky Luciano
🔺 Bonanno's alleged plot to kill Carlo Gambino, Tommy Lucchese and Stefano Magaddino
🔺 The betrayal of Joe Colombo
🔺 The night on Park Avenue: "Come on, Joe. My boss wants to see you."
🔺 19 months missing, and the Banana War in Brooklyn
🔺 The Troutman Street ambush, where hundreds of shots hit no one
🔺 The return at Foley Square, and why almost nobody believed him
🔺 The 1968 Tucson bombings, and the FBI agent behind them
🔺 The memoir that helped inspire the Commission case

Joseph Bonanno (1905–2002), known in the press as "Joe Bananas", ran one of New York's Five Families for more than three decades and sat on the Mafia Commission from 1931. In October 1964, the night before he was due before a federal grand jury, he vanished from Park Avenue. His family split in the Banana War between loyalists led by his son Bill Bonanno and the faction of Gaspar DiGregorio. He reappeared at the Foley Square federal courthouse in May 1966, claiming his cousin Stefano Magaddino had kidnapped him; most historians believe it was staged.

He retired to Tucson, Arizona, survived the 1968 bombing campaign organized by FBI agent David Hale, and in 1983 published "A Man of Honor", a book that helped inspire Rudy Giuliani's Commission case. He died in 2002, at 97.
