Before we wrap up, here are the five warning signs to watch for in 2027 – for any brand, not just the ones in this video.

One: sales collapsing month after month, not just one bad month. Two: dealers closing, especially in regional areas. Three: a change of importer or distributor – like Peugeot. Four: the model range shrinking, with popular models paused or axed and no clear replacements. And five: huge, ongoing discounts on new cars just to move stock.

One warning sign on its own doesn't mean a brand is leaving. But when you see three or four together, it's time to pay attention – especially if you're about to buy.

So, which car brands might not survive 2027 in Australia?

On the numbers, Jeep and Peugeot are under the most pressure – Jeep with sales down more than ninety per cent from its peak, and Peugeot with its importer walking away. Jaguar has deliberately stopped selling new cars while it reinvents itself. Alfa Romeo is going backwards. And while Mitsubishi, Subaru, Nissan and Mazda aren't going anywhere, they face falling sales and big emissions penalties.

But remember: none of these brands has announced it's leaving, and several say they're committed to Australia. In a market with around seventy brands, though, history tells us that not all of them will still be here in a few years.
