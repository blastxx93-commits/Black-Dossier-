Now, the Japanese brands. Let's be very clear: these brands are not about to leave Australia. They sell tens of thousands of cars a year, and they have huge dealer and service networks. But they're under more pressure than at any time in years – and that can affect prices, models and resale values.

Mitsubishi: 24,802 sales in the first half of 2026, down 25.7 per cent, according to CarExpert. In September 2026, it was down 41.5 per cent on the same month a year earlier. The good news for Mitsubishi is the all-new Pajero, due in showrooms from December 2026, priced from $68,490 before on-road costs.

Subaru: 14,817 sales in the first half, down 25.6 per cent. In September, down 42.6 per cent. Subaru's estimated emissions liability for the second half of 2025 was almost seven million dollars.

Nissan: 13,854 sales in the first half, with double-digit falls across its main models. Its estimated emissions liability was around $10.7 million. Nissan's Oceania managing director, Steve Milette, has openly said: "2026 is the low point." Nissan is betting on new models, including the plug-in hybrid Navara Pro for 2027 and the new V6 Patrol.

And Mazda: the biggest estimated emissions liability of any brand, at around $25.4 million for just six months. Mazda needs electric and hybrid cars, and fast – including the CX-6e electric SUV.

Why does this matter if you own one of these brands? Because when a brand is under pressure, it has to make tough choices. It might raise prices on its most polluting models to cover penalties. It might cut slow-selling models. Or it might slash prices to win back buyers – which helps new buyers, but can hurt the resale value of the cars people already own.
