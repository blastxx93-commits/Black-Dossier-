Number three: Jaguar.

This one is different, because Jaguar isn't really struggling with sales – it has deliberately stopped selling new cars. Jaguar stopped selling new petrol cars in Australia on the 31st of December 2025, as it prepares to relaunch as an electric luxury brand. That's why it sold zero new cars here in September 2026.

The new electric Jaguars are expected to cost around $300,000, according to Man of Many. And there's still no confirmed local launch date. Jaguar's local retailers are still servicing existing cars. But for a brand with nearly a century of history here, going from a full range to zero new cars is a huge gamble.

If you own a Jaguar, the practical questions are servicing, parts and resale. Existing Jaguars are still supported by local retailers. But with no new models on sale, there are fewer buyers walking into showrooms – and that can make selling a used Jaguar harder, at least until the new electric models arrive.

Number four: Alfa Romeo.

Alfa Romeo sold just twenty-one cars in Australia in September 2026. By May, its sales were down almost forty-seven per cent for the year. For a brand with such a passionate following, that's a tiny number. Alfa's range in Australia has been small for years, and when a brand only has a couple of models, one slow model can drag down the whole business. Like Jeep and Peugeot, it's part of Stellantis – and as we'll see, the whole Stellantis group in Australia is under pressure.
