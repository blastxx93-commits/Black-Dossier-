And then there's the group behind three of the brands on this list: Stellantis.

Stellantis owns Jeep, Peugeot, Alfa Romeo and Fiat, along with Ram, and it imports Chinese brand Leapmotor through a partnership. In June 2026, CarsGuide's Stephen Ottley wrote that Leapmotor sales were up more than 112 per cent, while Jeep, Peugeot, Alfa Romeo and Fiat were all going backwards. He asked the question directly: "does Jeep have a long-term future in Australia?" And he pointed out that Dodge and Chrysler had already left – and that General Motors said it was committed to Holden right up until it wasn't.

To be fair, Stellantis says it's investing heavily globally, with plans for 60 new vehicles and 50 updated models. And Jeep and Peugeot both say they're staying in Australia. But the numbers make it easy to see why owners are nervous.

So who's filling the gap? Mostly the newer brands. CarExpert reported Leapmotor up more than 150 per cent in the first half of 2026, to 779 sales. CarsGuide reported that BYD's luxury brand Denza had passed 1,600 sales since arriving. And more Chinese brands keep arriving, with new utes and SUVs planned for 2027. The market isn't shrinking – the brands at the top are simply changing.
