Let's start with the one in the most trouble on paper: Jeep.

In 2014, Jeep sold 30,408 vehicles in Australia. That was its peak. In 2024, it sold 2,456. In 2025, 1,682. And in the first half of 2026, CarExpert reported just 365 sales – down more than sixty-six per cent – with only twenty-eight in June. In September 2026, it sold sixteen.

The dealer network has shrunk too. CarExpert reported around forty-four Jeep retailers, down from more than seventy at its peak. And back in 2025, some Jeep dealers sought legal advice from the same lawyer who represented Holden dealers when General Motors closed Holden.

And look at what's left of the range. Jeep's current Australian line-up is the Avenger electric SUV, the Compass, the Wrangler and the Gladiator ute. The Grand Cherokee, which sold 16,582 cars in 2014, sold just 645 in 2024 – its last full year on sale – before it was paused.

Prices are part of the problem. In 2025, CarSauce reported that the cheapest four-door Wrangler had risen from about $37,000 in 2014 to $75,950 before on-road costs. And Jeep paused the Grand Cherokee in March 2025 – a model that sold more than 16,000 a year at its peak.

But Jeep insists it's staying. In April 2026, Jeep's vice-president of global product planning, Matt Nyquist, told CarExpert: "The right-hand drive markets are so important… we're committed to right-hand drive [and] Australia." Jeep says a new Compass is due in 2026, and the electric Recon is "on the horizon." Nyquist did acknowledge that plans can change, and that Jeep constantly reviews mature markets like Australia.
