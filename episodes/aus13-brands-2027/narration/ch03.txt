It's a sad story for a brand that was once one of the biggest names in Australian four-wheel driving. In the 2010s, a Grand Cherokee was a common sight in every suburb. Today, many Australians under thirty have probably never set foot in a Jeep showroom.

Number two: Peugeot.

Peugeot's best year in Australia was 2007, when it sold 8,807 vehicles. In 2025, it sold 1,350. In the first half of 2026, CarExpert reported 427 sales, down more than forty-one per cent. And in September 2026, it sold just eighty-nine.

And look at what Peugeot is actually selling. CarExpert reported that of Peugeot's 373 deliveries to the end of May 2026, 161 – about forty-three per cent – were the Partner van. Its passenger cars are barely registering. Among Stellantis brands in Australia, Peugeot was third this year, behind Ram with 1,257 sales and Fiat with 745.

Then, in July 2026, came the big blow. Inchcape, the company that has imported and distributed Peugeot in Australia, agreed with Stellantis to end the partnership. Inchcape had already dropped Peugeot's sister brand Citroen in 2024. No final date has been set.

Stellantis says Peugeot is staying. Its statement: "Peugeot has a strong future in Australia, supported by a robust product pipeline and a clear long-term strategy." It said it intends to "maintain continuity of distribution in Australia." But it hasn't said who will actually distribute the brand once Inchcape steps away.

In the meantime, Inchcape will keep selling existing stock and handling warranty and servicing for current owners. And Peugeot has slashed prices – in July 2026, The Beep reported the 5008 GT Premium Hybrid cut by ten thousand dollars, from $69,990 to $59,990 drive-away.
