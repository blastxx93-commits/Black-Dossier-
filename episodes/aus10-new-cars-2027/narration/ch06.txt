Number four: the cheaper Toyota RAV4 plug-in hybrid.

The RAV4 plug-in hybrid only arrived in Australia in mid-2026, and it took off immediately. According to CarExpert, plug-in hybrids made up thirty-nine per cent of all RAV4 sales in July 2026 – around 2,170 cars. That made the RAV4 PHEV the best-selling plug-in hybrid in the country that month, ahead of the BYD Shark 6 on 1,216 sales. CarExpert said that on its own, the RAV4 PHEV would have ranked sixth among all models that month.

Right now, the RAV4 PHEV starts at $58,840 before on-road costs for the XSE front-wheel drive. But in the first three months of 2027, Toyota is adding a cheaper GXL plug-in hybrid. Toyota hasn't announced the price yet. CarExpert estimated it could be around $53,990 before on-road costs, based on the gap between the hybrid and plug-in RAV4s. That's an estimate, not an official figure.

It'll use the same 201-kilowatt plug-in hybrid system, with a claimed electric range of 121 kilometres. For a lot of families, that's enough to do the school run and the commute without using any petrol at all.

Why does this matter so much? Because the RAV4 is one of the best-selling cars in Australia. And a cheaper plug-in version is Toyota's most direct answer to BYD, GWM, Chery and every other Chinese brand selling plug-in hybrid SUVs. It's also expected to undercut the Mitsubishi Outlander plug-in hybrid, which starts at $58,990 before on-road costs.
