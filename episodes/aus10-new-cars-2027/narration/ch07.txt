Number three: the Hyundai ute.

For years, Hyundai has watched Ford, Toyota and its sister brand Kia sell utes in Australia. Now it's finally building one. In July 2026, Hyundai Motor Company Australia's chief operating officer, Gavin Donaldson, said: "There's no doubt we'll be late to the party. That's the reality."

According to carsales, the dual-cab ute's reveal is expected in 2027, and industry sources suggest it could reach Australia by late 2027. And it won't be a traditional diesel. Donaldson said: "I think realistically, in our eyes, a hybrid or an EREV would be what we would be looking at." An EREV is an extended-range electric vehicle, where a petrol engine acts as a generator for an electric drivetrain.

And there's no doubt about the target. As Donaldson put it: "The benchmark in Australia historically over the last 10 years has been Ranger."

Number two: the GAC ute.

GAC is one of China's biggest car makers, and it only launched in Australia recently. In March 2026, carsales reported that GAC will launch a dual-cab ute in Australia in 2027. It's expected to look similar to the Pickup 01 concept shown at the 2025 Shanghai motor show, with a ladder frame chassis and a petrol-electric powertrain – either a plug-in hybrid or a range extender.

GAC International Australia's CEO, Kevin Shu, didn't hold back. He said: "I think we'll have a future advantage to win the competition with Ranger, HiLux, and so on like this." He also said the ute needs to balance commercial and passenger use – which is exactly how most Australians use their dual-cabs.

And they'll be joining a crowded field. Chery has a diesel-hybrid ute due before the end of 2026, with a petrol-hybrid version expected in 2027. JAC, GWM, BYD, KGM and Deepal are already selling electrified utes here.
