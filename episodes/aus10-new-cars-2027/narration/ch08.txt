And number one: the Nissan Navara Pro plug-in hybrid.

This is the one that really caught my attention. In October 2026, Nissan confirmed a plug-in hybrid version of the next Navara for Australia in 2027. The preliminary figures are huge: 300 kilowatts and 800 newton-metres, from a 2.0-litre turbo petrol engine combined with a high-output electric motor and an eight-speed automatic.

Nissan is targeting 3,500 kilograms of braked towing, and a payload of up to 1,000 kilograms. For comparison, CarExpert listed the BYD Shark 6 Performance at a 725-kilogram payload, and the Ford Ranger Hybrid Wildtrak at 901 kilograms. CarExpert also listed the GWM Cannon Alpha plug-in hybrid at 685 kilograms, the Chery Stockman at 765 kilograms with 3,495 kilograms of towing, and the JAC T9 plug-in hybrid at 915 kilograms. So on paper, the Navara Pro plug-in hybrid could out-carry all of its main rivals.

There are still some big unknowns. Nissan hasn't announced the price, the battery size or the electric-only range. Those will come closer to launch.

But here's why it's number one. Utes are the most important segment in Australia, and they're the furthest behind on emissions. The Ford Ranger and the Toyota HiLux are still mostly sold as diesels. A plug-in hybrid ute with more power than a Raptor, full towing capacity and a one-tonne payload is exactly the kind of vehicle that could convince tradies and farmers that electrified utes can do the job.

And Nissan says Australia helped design it. Nissan Oceania's managing director, Steve Milette, said: "Australia wasn't simply where we tested Navara Pro PHEV. It was one of the markets helping shape it."
