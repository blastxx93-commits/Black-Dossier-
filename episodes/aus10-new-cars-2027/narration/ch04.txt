Number six: the Omoda 4 and Omoda 7.

Omoda Jaecoo is one of the fastest-growing brands in Australia, and in September 2026 it confirmed two new SUVs for the first half of 2027. The Omoda 4 is a compact SUV aimed at younger buyers. The Omoda 7 is a mid-size SUV that sits below the Omoda 9.

Both will offer what the brand calls new energy powertrains. Carsales has reported the Omoda 7 plug-in hybrid with around 255 kilowatts and 525 newton-metres, with an electric range of around ninety kilometres on the WLTP cycle. No Australian prices have been confirmed yet.

Omoda Jaecoo Australia's chief commercial officer, Roy Munoz, said "Omoda was created to challenge convention." And that's the threat. These brands are aiming straight at the Toyota RAV4, the Mazda CX-5 and the Hyundai Tucson, often with more equipment for less money.

Number five: the Mitsubishi Pajero.

Yes, it's back. The Pajero was one of the most famous four-wheel drives in Australian history, and Mitsubishi stopped selling it here in 2021. Now there's an all-new 2027 Pajero, with a body-on-frame design and a new 2.4-litre turbo-diesel making 150 kilowatts and 480 newton-metres, with an eight-speed automatic and Mitsubishi's Super Select four-wheel-drive system.

Carsales reported prices from $68,490 for the five-seat GLS, up to $84,990 for the GSR, before on-road costs. It's due in showrooms from December 2026, so it'll really hit Australian roads in 2027. Mitsubishi Australia's CEO Shunichi Kihara said: "For four generations, the Pajero has defined Mitsubishi."

Mitsubishi calls it an all-new Pajero, and Kihara said it "takes the Pajero backstory and modernises it." It's also a sign of how much Australians still love big diesel four-wheel drives for towing and touring – even as the emissions rules get tougher.

Its main target is the Toyota Prado, which carsales listed at $73,200 to $100,620. If Mitsubishi can undercut the Prado with a tough, capable diesel four-wheel drive, a lot of Prado buyers will at least take a look.
