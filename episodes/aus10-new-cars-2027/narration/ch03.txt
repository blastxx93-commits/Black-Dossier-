Number eight: the Mazda CX-6e.

Mazda has had a tough time under the new emissions rules. In the first six months of the scheme, its estimated liability was around $25.4 million – the biggest of any brand. So Mazda needs electric cars, and fast.

The CX-6e is an electric SUV with about 190 kilowatts and rear-wheel drive. CarsGuide listed it as a 2027 model with an estimated price of around fifty-four thousand dollars. Again, that's an estimate. But if Mazda can bring its usual quality and style to a properly priced electric SUV, it could finally give loyal Mazda buyers a reason to go electric without switching brands.

For a brand that's built its reputation on petrol engines and driving feel, the CX-6e is a big test. If it sells well, it could pull Mazda back into credit under the emissions rules. If it doesn't, Mazda's penalty bill could keep growing.

Number seven: the BYD Atto 3 Evo.

The Atto 3 was the car that launched BYD in Australia. Now it's getting a major update. According to CarsGuide, the 2027 Atto 3 Evo will come in rear-wheel drive and all-wheel drive, with the flagship producing 330 kilowatts. That's an enormous amount of power for a small SUV.

And BYD keeps moving fast. In 2026 alone, it launched the budget Atto 1, grew its Shark 6 ute into one of the best-selling 4x4 utes in the country, and built up more than six million credits under the emissions rules – more than any other brand.

BYD is already the second best-selling brand in Australia. In September 2026, it sold 8,191 cars. A much more powerful, updated Atto 3 will put even more pressure on the Toyota Corolla Cross, the Mazda CX-30 and every other small SUV in the country.
