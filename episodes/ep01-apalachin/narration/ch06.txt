CHAPTER SIX. Visiting a sick friend.

At the barracks, the troopers asked each man the same simple question.

Why are you in Apalachin?

And one by one, these men, from different cities, different families, different businesses, gave remarkably similar answers.

They had come to visit an old friend. Joseph Barbara had a heart condition. They had heard he was unwell. They stopped by to see how he was doing.

It was true that Barbara had suffered a heart attack the year before. But the idea that dozens of men had all, by coincidence, chosen the same Thursday to check on his health was hard to take seriously.

Some described themselves as businessmen. Olive oil importers. Dress manufacturers. Restaurant owners. Taxi company operators.

Together, according to later reports, the men had criminal histories adding up to more than two hundred and seventy-five arrests and over one hundred convictions.

And yet, the officers faced a real problem.

Attending a barbecue was not a crime. Visiting a friend was not a crime. Driving across state lines was not a crime. Running from a party was embarrassing, but it was not, by itself, a crime.

Croswell had their names. He had their faces. He had their addresses and their license plates.

What he didn't have was a charge that would hold.

So, after they were identified and questioned, the men were released.

It looked like a defeat.

It wasn't.
