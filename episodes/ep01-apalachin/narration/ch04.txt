CHAPTER FOUR. Room reservations.

On November 13th, the day before the meeting, Sergeant Croswell was at the Parkway Motel in the nearby town of Vestal, on an unrelated police matter.

While he was there, he noticed a familiar young man crossing the parking lot. It was Joseph Barbara Junior, the son of the man Croswell had been watching for years.

The younger Barbara was reserving rooms. Several of them. For out-of-town guests.

For most police officers, that would mean nothing. Families book rooms. Businesses host visitors.

But Croswell knew who Joseph Barbara was. And he knew who had visited that house before.

Then came another detail. Around the same time, Croswell learned that a large meat order had been placed with a local butcher for the Barbara home. Roughly two hundred pounds of steaks and chops.

Rooms for out-of-town guests. A feast big enough for an army. At the home of a man Croswell believed was a regional crime boss.

Croswell didn't have a crime. He didn't have a warrant. What he had was a hunch, and a pair of eyes.

So the next morning, he decided to go and take a look.
