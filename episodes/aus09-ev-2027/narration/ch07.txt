And what about a national charge?

In 2025, Treasurer Jim Chalmers said the government was "accelerating work" on an EV road user charge. But in April 2026, Transport Minister Catherine King told the ABC she wasn't sure there was a path to pass one through parliament, and that "it may not be the time for it right now." The May 2026 federal budget didn't include one. The government said it was "continuing to work with states and territories."

So in 2027, you could end up with one state charging EV drivers and the rest of the country not – unless Canberra steps in.

The reaction has been strong on both sides. A NSW Government spokesperson argued that "everybody who uses roads should contribute to them." The NRMA and the Electric Vehicle Council have both supported a road user charge in principle – but the Electric Vehicle Council argued it should only start once EVs make up thirty per cent of the national fleet. Its chief executive, Julie Delvecchio, said the NSW tax "fails on all three fronts." The council cited Sydney University research finding twenty-eight per cent of likely EV buyers would be dissuaded, and pointed to New Zealand, where it said the EV share of sales fell from 20.6 per cent to 3.8 per cent after a similar move. Polestar Australia's boss Scott Maynard called a national charge on EVs a "thinly veiled tax."

It's worth putting those numbers side by side. An average EV driver in NSW might pay a few hundred dollars a year under the proposed charge. A petrol car doing the same distance pays a similar amount, or more, in fuel excise – plus the much higher cost of the fuel itself. So even with a road user charge, running an EV on home charging is still likely to be cheaper for most people. The question is whether the charge arrives before EVs are affordable for everyone.

My take? Some form of road user charge for EVs looks very likely in the long run, because fuel excise is shrinking as more people go electric. The real fight is about when it starts, how much it is, and whether it's fair compared with what petrol and diesel drivers pay.
