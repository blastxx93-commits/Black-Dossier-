So what does this mean for you?

If you're looking at an EV under $75,000, the change on the 1st of April 2027 doesn't affect you – at least until 2029. That covers a huge chunk of the market now. In February 2026, CarsGuide listed seven electric cars available for under forty thousand dollars drive-away, including the BYD Dolphin, the MG4, the GWM Ora, the Chery E5, the Leapmotor B10 and the Hyundai Inster. And the BYD Atto 1 started from $23,990 before on-road costs.

But if you're looking at an EV between $75,000 and the luxury car tax threshold – think larger SUVs and premium models – the date matters a lot. Sign an eligible novated lease before the 1st of April 2027, and you lock in the full exemption. Sign after, and you only get the twenty-five per cent discount.

That's why I'd expect a rush of more expensive EV leases in the first three months of 2027. And it's why you should talk to your employer and your lease provider now, rather than in March, when everyone else is trying to do the same thing.

One warning: delivery times. A lease generally needs the car to actually be provided to you for the benefit to apply, so check with your provider how the timing works. If the car you want has a long wait, ordering in late March might be too late.
