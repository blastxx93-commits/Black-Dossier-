Now, the second change: the luxury car tax.

Right now, cars over a certain price pay an extra tax of thirty-three per cent on every dollar above the threshold. For fuel-efficient vehicles, including electric cars, that threshold is $91,387.

On the 24th of March 2026, Prime Minister Anthony Albanese announced a free trade deal with the European Union. As part of it, Australia agreed to remove the five per cent tariff on European passenger cars. And CarExpert reported a new luxury car tax category for zero-emissions vehicles, with a threshold of $120,000. According to CarExpert, that new zero-emissions threshold is due to start on the 1st of July 2027. The trade deal still needs to be formally ratified.

What does that mean in dollars? Here's a simple illustration. Today, an EV priced at $100,000 is $8,613 over the threshold. At thirty-three per cent, that's about $2,842 in luxury car tax. Under a $120,000 threshold, the same car would pay no luxury car tax at all.

But don't get too excited. James Voortman, chief executive of the Australian Automotive Dealer Association, welcomed the trade deal but described the luxury car tax change as "a narrow change that will only affect less than one per cent of vehicles sold." So this helps buyers of premium EVs – think Polestar, BMW, Mercedes-Benz, Porsche and Audi – much more than buyers of an everyday BYD or MG.
