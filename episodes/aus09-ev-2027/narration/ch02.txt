Here's exactly how it works.

Stage one: until the 31st of March 2027, nothing changes. EVs under the fuel-efficient luxury car tax threshold keep the full exemption.

Stage two: from the 1st of April 2027 to the 31st of March 2029, the full exemption only applies to EVs costing $75,000 or less. Electric cars above $75,000, but still under the luxury car tax threshold, get a twenty-five per cent discount on their fringe benefits tax instead of a full exemption.

Stage three: from the 1st of April 2029, all eligible EVs get the twenty-five per cent discount. The full exemption is gone.

And there are two important details. First, existing leases are protected. If you're already in an eligible novated lease before the change, you're grandfathered. What does "grandfathered" mean in practice? It means the rules that applied when your lease started keep applying for that lease. You don't suddenly lose the exemption halfway through. But when that lease ends and you take out a new one, the new rules will apply.

Second, plug-in hybrids already lost their exemption on the 1st of April 2025. So this is only about fully electric cars.

The NRMA estimated that some novated lease EV owners could lose more than seventeen thousand dollars in tax savings under the changes. Your own number depends on your salary, the car's price, the lease length and the kilometres you drive.

The Electric Vehicle Council welcomed the fact that the discount was extended rather than scrapped outright. Its chief executive, Julie Delvecchio, said it was "good news for everyday Australians who are doing the sums on going electric."
