Now, the change that's causing the biggest argument: paying per kilometre.

Every time you fill up with petrol or diesel, you pay fuel excise. That money goes to the federal budget, and a large share is spent on roads. EV drivers don't buy fuel, so they don't pay it.

And fuel excise has been in the spotlight in 2026. During the fuel crisis, the federal government temporarily halved it, from 52.6 cents a litre to 26.3 cents, from the 1st of April until the 1st of July. The NRMA also noted that the share of fuel excise reinvested in roads fell to seventy per cent in the 2026 budget, down from seventy-nine per cent forecast a year earlier. So the question of who pays for roads is only getting bigger. Chasing Cars estimated that EVs cost the federal government roughly $130 million to $165 million a year in lost fuel excise.

New South Wales plans to fix that with a road user charge from July 2027. Chasing Cars reported the proposed rates as about 2.974 cents per kilometre for electric vehicles and 2.379 cents per kilometre for plug-in hybrids. AAP reported it was forecast to raise $440 million in its first three years.

What would that cost you? At 12,000 to 15,000 kilometres a year, Chasing Cars calculated an EV driver would pay about $360 to $450. For comparison, a petrol car using seven litres per hundred kilometres would pay around $440 to $550 a year in fuel excise over the same distance.

And notice something: plug-in hybrids would pay the charge too, even though they also burn petrol and pay fuel excise when they do. That's one of the reasons critics say the design needs work.

But there's a legal problem. Victoria introduced a similar charge – 2.8 cents per kilometre for EVs – and in 2023 the High Court found that only the federal government can levy that kind of tax. So how can NSW go ahead?

NSW Treasurer Daniel Mookhey has said NSW would prefer a national system. But, quote, "in the absence of a national system, it remains the intent of the NSW government to enact the law." He suggested the Commonwealth could refer its powers to NSW to avoid another court defeat. As of mid-2026, NSW hadn't said exactly how it would collect the money.
