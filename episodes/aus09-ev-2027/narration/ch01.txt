Let's start with the big one: the Electric Car Discount.

Since 2022, electric cars provided by an employer, including through a novated lease, have been exempt from fringe benefits tax – as long as they're under the luxury car tax threshold for fuel-efficient vehicles. That threshold is currently $91,387.

If you've never used one, here's how a novated lease works in simple terms. It's an agreement between you, your employer and a leasing company. Your employer pays the car's costs – the lease, and often running costs like insurance, servicing and charging – out of your salary before income tax is taken out. That lowers your taxable income. Normally, that benefit attracts fringe benefits tax, which eats into the saving. For eligible EVs, that tax has been zero.

For a lot of Australians, this has been the single biggest reason to buy an EV. A novated lease lets you pay for the car out of your pre-tax salary, and with no fringe benefits tax on top, the savings can be huge. The National Automotive Leasing and Salary Packaging Association's chief executive, Rohan Martin, has called it "Australia's most effective demand-side lever for EV uptake."

But it's been expensive for the government. A statutory review found that "the revenue costs of the Electric Car Discount are increasing significantly", and that "alternative policy approaches may be more fiscally efficient and equitable."

And it's been popular. Leasing industry groups have pointed to the discount as a major reason EV sales took off, especially among middle-income workers who could suddenly afford a new car they'd never have considered paying cash for.

So in May 2026, the government confirmed the discount would be wound back in three stages.
