Now, here's the change that could actually make EVs cheaper in 2027: the emissions rules.

Under the New Vehicle Efficiency Standard, the target for passenger cars drops from 117 grams of carbon dioxide per kilometre in 2026 to 92 grams in 2027. That's a cut of more than twenty per cent in a single year. Then 68 grams in 2028, and 58 in 2029.

Car makers that miss their targets build up penalties of roughly fifty dollars per gram per vehicle. The best way to avoid them is to sell more electric and hybrid cars. And the fastest way to sell more EVs is to cut prices.

We've already seen it. Hyundai cut up to $13,857 from some versions of the Kona Electric in 2026. And CarsGuide reported that the government's Clean Energy Finance Corporation committed sixty million dollars to Hyundai Capital Australia to help offer cheaper finance on Hyundai and Kia EVs.

And it isn't just about price cuts. The tougher the targets get, the more EV and hybrid models car makers need to bring to Australia. More than sixty new EV models were expected to enter the Australian market as the scheme started. More choice usually means more competition, and more competition usually means better deals.

The market is already moving fast. The Australian Automobile Association reported that battery electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles made up 31.61 per cent of new light vehicle sales in the June quarter of 2026. By September 2026, battery electric cars made up more than twenty-four per cent of new car sales. CarExpert reported that EVs outsold both petrol and diesel vehicles in August and September 2026. And as we covered in our last video, the federal opposition has promised to scrap the NVES if it wins government – so this part of the picture could change too.

And there's one more change from the 2026 budget worth knowing about. The government committed forty million dollars to new regional and kerbside chargers. That matters, because a lack of charging is still one of the biggest reasons people give for not buying an EV, especially outside the big cities.
