Here's the interesting part. Tesla isn't struggling in Australia. It's the Cybertruck that didn't fit.

In September 2026, Tesla sold 5,654 cars in Australia, up more than twenty-one per cent on the same month last year. Electric vehicles made up 24.2 per cent of all new car sales that month – more than petrol cars, for the second month in a row. And with a fuel crisis pushing diesel towards three dollars a litre in some capital cities, more Aussies than ever are thinking about going electric.

You can see it in the insurance data too. NRMA Insurance says requests for electric vehicle insurance quotes were up eighty per cent in the first nine months of 2026. And NRMA's own survey found the share of Australians actively considering a battery electric car rose from twenty per cent in 2024 to thirty-one per cent in 2026. The demand for electric vehicles in Australia is real. It just isn't demand for a $130,000 stainless steel truck.

In fact, 2026 has been a record year for Tesla here. In June, Tesla sold 8,670 vehicles in Australia – the strongest month in its local history, according to Electric Vehicle Council figures. The Model Y alone sold 8,072 that month, and 20,396 in the first half of the year – up more than ninety-five per cent. Tesla's first-half total was 23,588, up almost sixty-seven per cent. A big part of that came from the new six-seat Model Y L.

It's not all good news. The Model 3 has gone backwards, down forty-seven per cent in June. And BYD's Sealion 7 has become a serious rival, with 7,786 deliveries through May. But overall, Tesla is having one of its best years ever in Australia.

So Tesla's message is clear. It would rather put its effort into the Model Y and Model 3 – cars that already sell in big numbers here – than into a niche, left-hand-drive truck that would cost a fortune to convert.

That's a business decision. But for the people who waited seven years, it's still a disappointment. And it raises a bigger question: if not the Cybertruck, then what?
