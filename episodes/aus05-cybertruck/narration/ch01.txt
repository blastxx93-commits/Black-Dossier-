To understand why this hurts, you have to go back to the start.

In November 2019, Elon Musk walked on stage in Los Angeles and revealed the Cybertruck. Stainless steel body. Armoured glass – which famously cracked on stage when a metal ball was thrown at it. And a promised starting price in the United States of just $39,900, with the top tri-motor version promising more than 500 miles – around 800 kilometres – of range.

The internet went wild. Reservations opened almost immediately, including in Australia, with a $150 refundable deposit. Tesla has said tens of thousands of people reserved between 2019 and 2021.

For Aussies, the idea made sense. We love utes. We love towing. And here was an electric pickup that promised to do it all – with a futuristic look nothing else on the road could match.

But there was a problem from day one. The Cybertruck was designed as a left-hand-drive American truck. Australia drives on the left, and our cars need the steering wheel on the right. Converting a vehicle like this isn't a quick job. It means re-engineering the dashboard, the steering, the pedals, and testing it all again.

And when the Cybertruck finally reached American customers at the end of 2023 – four years after the reveal – it was more expensive and had less range than originally promised. The dream and the reality had already started to drift apart.
