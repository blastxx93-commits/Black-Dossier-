Now let's talk about the vehicle Australians were really hoping the Cybertruck would be: an electric ute.

Here's the reality in 2026. Electric utes make up less than one per cent of Australian ute sales. According to CarExpert, there were 157,531 ute deliveries to September, and diesel and plug-in hybrids still dominate.

Toyota launched the HiLux BEV, the electric version of the country's most famous ute. But it sold roughly 300 between May and early July, with fewer than ten going to private buyers. KGM's Musso EV has sold just over 300 since its launch late last year. And Isuzu has cancelled its plans to bring the D-Max EV to Australia altogether. As Isuzu's Rod Caldwell put it: "Customers don't want it."

Not everyone agrees. In September 2026, MG confirmed the U9 EV, an all-electric dual-cab ute, priced from $78,990 before on-road costs. It claims 430 kilometres of range, 325 kilowatts of power, and – crucially for Aussie buyers – 3,500 kilograms of braked towing. It can charge from twenty to eighty per cent in about forty-two minutes on a fast charger, and carry a payload of 685 kilograms. Deliveries start in the last quarter of 2026. MG says customers here have been "yearning" for a vehicle like this.

And then there's the plug-in hybrid middle ground, led by the BYD Shark 6, which was the third best-selling 4x4 pick-up in Australia across the first half of 2026 – a remarkable result for a ute that only arrived at the start of 2025. GAC has confirmed its upcoming ute will be electrified only, and Chery's Stockman plug-in hybrid is due late in 2026.

So the electric ute is coming. It just doesn't look like a stainless steel spaceship.
