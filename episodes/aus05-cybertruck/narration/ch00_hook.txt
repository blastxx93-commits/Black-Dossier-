One hundred and fifty dollars. That's what thousands of Australians paid in November 2019 to reserve a spot in the queue for the most talked-about vehicle on the planet – the Tesla Cybertruck.

They waited through a pandemic. They waited while the reservation page quietly disappeared in 2022. They watched Tesla parade a Cybertruck around Australian showrooms in 2024. They heard Tesla's local boss say he was personally confident it would come.

Then, on Friday the 23rd of October 2026 – almost seven years after they paid – the email arrived. Quote: "After careful consideration, plans to produce Cybertruck for Australia and New Zealand have been discontinued."

That's it. No Cybertruck. Not now. Not ever, according to Tesla's own plans.

So what actually happened? Why did Tesla walk away from Australia? What does the refund look like? And with the Cybertruck gone, which electric and American utes can Aussies actually buy in 2026? Stay with me, because the real reason the Cybertruck died says a lot about where the whole ute market is heading.
