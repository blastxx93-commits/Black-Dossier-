First, let's look at what's actually happening, because the numbers from September 2026 are remarkable.

Australians bought 108,778 new vehicles in September. That's up slightly on the same month last year. So the market isn't collapsing. People are still buying cars. What's changing is who they're buying from.

Look at the brands that grew. BYD, up sixty-one per cent, now Australia's second best-selling brand for the year. Geely, up 870 per cent. Omoda and Jaecoo, up 344 per cent. Zeekr, up more than sixteen hundred per cent. Leapmotor more than doubled.

Now look at the other side. Even big, established names went backwards. Kia, down nineteen and a half per cent in September. Mazda, down nearly nineteen. Hyundai, down twenty-two. Among the top ten brands, the only ones that grew were Tesla and the Chinese brands.

There's another big shift too. In September, electric vehicles made up 24.2 per cent of all new car sales – more than petrol cars, for the second month in a row. Petrol sales fell thirty-six per cent year-on-year. Diesel fell eighteen. If your range is mostly petrol and diesel, 2026 is a very hard year to be selling cars.

And the market is crowded. Around seventy brands are now fighting for roughly 1.2 million sales a year. Every sale a new brand wins comes out of someone else's pocket. So why is this happening now? There are three big forces hitting at once.

The first is price. Chinese brands arrived with well-equipped hybrids and EVs at prices the established brands struggle to match. When a family can get a plug-in hybrid SUV with a big screen and a long warranty for the same money as a basic petrol SUV, plenty of them are making the switch.

The second is fuel. Petrol and diesel are losing share every month, and the New Vehicle Efficiency Standard now pushes car makers to sell lower-emission vehicles or pay penalties. Brands with old, thirsty ranges are being squeezed from both sides – by buyers and by the rules.

The third is the sheer number of choices. A few years ago, Australia had around fifty brands. Now there are about seventy. A dealer body boss has already warned that more brands will leave. The pie isn't getting much bigger. It's just being cut into thinner and thinner slices.
