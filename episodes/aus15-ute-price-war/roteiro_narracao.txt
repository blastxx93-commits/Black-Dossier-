### ch00_hook
Four thousand dollars off a Toyota HiLux. CarsGuide called it a "rare" move – because Toyota almost never needs to discount Australia's most famous ute. But in October 2026, that's exactly what happened.

And Toyota isn't alone. Ford has cut its Ranger plug-in hybrid by more than ten thousand dollars and handed out four-thousand-dollar fuel cards. Kia has slashed up to fifteen thousand dollars off its Tasman. BYD is offering four thousand dollars cashback on the Shark 6. Australia's ute market – the most profitable, most fiercely defended part of the whole car industry – is in a full-blown price war.

Why now? Because Australians are buying fewer traditional utes – and the brands know it. Sales of traditional utes and vans were down more than thirteen per cent in September, and down more than ten per cent for the year. Some of the biggest names are going backwards by thirty, forty, even fifty per cent.

In this video, I'll show you who's cutting prices, by how much, and why. I'll show you which utes are winning and which are getting crushed. And at the end, I'll tell you how to get the best possible deal – and the traps hidden inside some of these offers.

### ch01
Let's start with the numbers, because they're the reason all of this is happening.

In September 2026, the Ford Ranger was Australia's best-selling vehicle, with 5,062 sales, up four per cent. But the Toyota HiLux fell to fourth, with 4,455 – down 11.7 per cent. Even more telling, the Toyota RAV4 SUV, with 4,624 sales, outsold the HiLux. Not long ago, that would have been unthinkable.

For the year to date, the race for Australia's number one vehicle is incredibly tight. The Ranger is on 37,858. The HiLux is on 36,616. That's a gap of just over twelve hundred, with three months to go. And winning that crown matters enormously for marketing, for dealers and for pride.

Back in May, CarsGuide's news editor, Dom Tripolone, put it bluntly: "We've hit peak ute and the only way is down." Four-wheel-drive ute sales were already falling then, and the decline has continued.

It's a remarkable turnaround. For most of the past decade, the Ranger and the HiLux took turns as the country's best-selling vehicle. Toyota's former sales and marketing boss, Sean Hanley, saw this coming back at the start of 2025. He said "the number of ute models available to Australian buyers will expand rapidly", and that an SUV could rise to the top of the national sales charts "in the next year or two". He was right on both counts.

So what's driving buyers away? Three big things.

First, fuel. In May 2026, diesel reached as high as $3.50 a litre in some parts of Australia, according to CarExpert. Fill an eighty-litre ute tank at that price and you're up for about $280.

Second, the government's emissions rules. Under the New Vehicle Efficiency Standard, car makers face penalties for selling too many high-emitting vehicles – and diesel utes are some of the highest emitters on the market.

Third, the alternatives have exploded. Plug-in hybrid utes, Chinese utes, and family SUVs with hybrid and electric power. Chinese-built vehicles made up more than thirty-five per cent of all new-car sales in September. Buyers who once defaulted to a dual-cab now have a dozen other options.

### ch02
So let's look at the deals, starting with the big one: Toyota.

Toyota is offering a $4,000 "deposit bonus" on new and demonstrator 2026 HiLux SR5 models – the four-wheel-drive versions. CarsGuide reported that the SR5 range starts at $69,901 drive-away in New South Wales, rising to $73,952 for the top double-cab cab-chassis with the new 48-volt mild-hybrid system.

But read the fine print. The bonus only applies if you finance the ute through Toyota Finance, with at least ten thousand dollars borrowed. Finance applications have to be in between the 1st of September and the 31st of December 2026, and the car has to be settled by the 28th of February 2027. It covers private buyers and some smaller fleet customers, and it excludes the electric HiLux.

Why is Toyota doing this? Its sales boss, John Pappas, said Toyota is "targeting a very strong, record fourth quarter sales for 2026," thanks to better vehicle supply. In plain English: Toyota has more utes coming, it wants to move them, and it wants to stop the Ranger from running away with the title.

And remember, this is a brand-new HiLux. The new generation arrived in late 2025, with a mild-hybrid diesel and even a fully electric version. Normally, a new model sells strongly in its first year. Instead, the HiLux was down almost twelve per cent in September. Toyota needs this one to work.

That's worth remembering. When the market leader starts offering finance bonuses on its most iconic model, it's not doing it for fun.

### ch03
Now Ford.

Ford's Ranger has been Australia's best-selling vehicle in 2024 and 2025, and it's leading again in 2026. But even the champion has been cutting prices.

In April 2026, Ford slashed the price of the Ranger plug-in hybrid – now simply called the Ranger Hybrid. The XLT Hybrid dropped to $62,000 drive-away. It had been listed at $71,990 before on-road costs, so Ford described that as over ten thousand dollars less. The Sport was $66,000 drive-away, the Wildtrak $70,000, and the Stormtrak $73,000.

At the same time, Ford handed out a $4,000 fuel card on 2026-model Rangers and entry-level Everests, timed with the federal government's cut to fuel excise. Ford's Australian boss, Fadi Mawal, said the offers were about helping customers through "short-term pain at the pump". Farmers registered as primary producers got an extra thousand dollars off any Ranger, including the hybrid and the big Super Duty.

And buyers responded. After the Wildtrak hybrid dropped to $70,000 drive-away, Ford said dealer stock was soon "all but exhausted". As a Ford spokesperson put it: "Australian buyers are doing the maths." That's the key lesson of this whole price war. Cut the price far enough, and buyers come back – even in a falling market.

Here's the interesting part. Ford is discounting from a position of strength. The Ranger is still growing. Ford isn't panicking about the Ranger itself – it's making sure that when Toyota finally swings back, there's no opening.

### ch04
And then there's the ute that's really struggling: the Kia Tasman.

When Kia launched the Tasman in 2025, it was the most important new ute in years – Kia's first ever, designed with Australia in mind. Kia forecast 20,000 sales in its first calendar year, and seven to ten per cent of the segment. It also expected half of those sales to go to fleets. Instead, Kia says the split is roughly one quarter fleet and three quarters private buyers – so the big company orders it was counting on haven't arrived.

The reality? From launch to the end of September 2026, Kia sold 7,898 Tasmans in total. In September, it found 355 buyers – down 56 per cent on the same month last year.

So Kia went hard on price. In May 2026, it cut the Tasman by up to $15,000. The top X-Pro dropped to $64,990 drive-away. The X-Line came down to $59,990, and the dual-cab S 4x4 to $49,990.

Kia's own executives have been remarkably honest. Then-boss Damien Meredith said the exterior is "very polarising". He admitted: "We've fallen short. We know the facts." But he also said, "once you're inside it, it's fantastic." Meredith also pointed out that the Tasman was planned six years ago, and the competition since then has become, in his words, "quite dramatic". And at the end of October, Kia's new chief executive, Dennis Piccoli, said Kia can do a "vastly better job" selling the Tasman, adding: "We'd like to sell more cars. It's as simple as that."

So the ute market isn't just shrinking. It's splitting. Buyers are going either cheaper, electrified, or much bigger – and the traditional mid-range diesel dual-cab is getting squeezed from all sides.

And there's more coming. The JAC Hunter plug-in hybrid has been targeting less than fifty thousand dollars drive-away. Chery has a diesel-electric ute due before the end of 2026. More choice means more competition – and more pressure on prices.

### ch08
Now here's the twist – and it's why the timing of your purchase matters so much.

These discounts might not last. In fact, Toyota itself has warned that ute prices could go up in 2027.

Under the New Vehicle Efficiency Standard, the emissions target for utes and vans is 180 grams of CO2 per kilometre in 2026. In 2027, it drops to 150. CarsGuide reported the HiLux SR5 with Toyota's mild-hybrid diesel is claimed at 191 grams per kilometre – already above the 2026 limit, and 41 grams over the 2027 one.

Toyota's John Pappas has called the 2027 targets for light commercial vehicles "really challenging". And Toyota said it "cannot rule out" price increases. That's a warning, not a price list – nobody has announced a 2027 price rise yet. And the first actual penalty bills, for vehicles sold in 2025, aren't due to be determined until February 2028, so car makers still have time to trade credits. But it tells you which way the pressure is pushing.

There's another possibility too. On the 29th of September 2026, the federal Coalition pledged to repeal the NVES if it wins government, calling it a "family car and ute tax". So the rules themselves could change. The point is simple: the conditions creating today's deals are unusual, and they might not be here in a year.

### ch09
So how do you get the best deal in the middle of a ute price war?

First, compare drive-away prices, not headlines. A "$4,000 bonus" and a "$4,000 fuel card" aren't the same as $4,000 off the price. Ask every dealer for a written drive-away price, including all accessories you actually need.

Second, read the finance conditions. The HiLux bonus requires Toyota Finance and a minimum amount borrowed. That can still be a good deal – but compare the interest rate and the total cost of the loan with your bank. A bonus that's swallowed by a higher rate isn't a bonus.

Third, check the build date and look at demonstrators. Toyota's offer covers demonstrators, and many brands have runout stock. Older build dates should mean a sharper price.

Fourth, think about resale. Toyota and Ford utes have historically held their value strongly. A heavily discounted ute from a brand with weaker resale might cost you more when you sell. Look at the total cost over the years you'll keep it, not just the price on day one.

Fifth, use the calendar. Black Friday falls on the 27th of November, BYD's cashback runs to the 21st of December, and the HiLux finance window closes on the 31st of December. Dealers chasing end-of-year targets are often at their most flexible in the final weeks of December.

And finally, buy the ute you actually need. If you tow a heavy caravan every weekend, a cheap plug-in hybrid with a lower towing capacity might not suit you, however good the deal. If you mostly drive in the suburbs, ask yourself whether you need a ute at all.

So should you buy now, or wait? If you need a ute in the next few months, this is one of the strongest buyer's markets in years. Toyota, Ford, Kia and BYD are all chasing volume before the end of 2026, and some of these deals end in December. But if you can wait, keep an eye on 2027. New plug-in and electric utes are arriving, and if the emissions rules push prices up, the deals could dry up quickly. Nobody can promise which way prices will go – but you'll be in a much stronger position if you know the numbers before you walk into a dealership.
