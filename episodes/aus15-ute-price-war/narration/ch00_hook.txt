Four thousand dollars off a Toyota HiLux. CarsGuide called it a "rare" move – because Toyota almost never needs to discount Australia's most famous ute. But in October 2026, that's exactly what happened.

And Toyota isn't alone. Ford has cut its Ranger plug-in hybrid by more than ten thousand dollars and handed out four-thousand-dollar fuel cards. Kia has slashed up to fifteen thousand dollars off its Tasman. BYD is offering four thousand dollars cashback on the Shark 6. Australia's ute market – the most profitable, most fiercely defended part of the whole car industry – is in a full-blown price war.

Why now? Because Australians are buying fewer traditional utes – and the brands know it. Sales of traditional utes and vans were down more than thirteen per cent in September, and down more than ten per cent for the year. Some of the biggest names are going backwards by thirty, forty, even fifty per cent.
