Now here's the twist – and it's why the timing of your purchase matters so much.

These discounts might not last. In fact, Toyota itself has warned that ute prices could go up in 2027.

Under the New Vehicle Efficiency Standard, the emissions target for utes and vans is 180 grams of CO2 per kilometre in 2026. In 2027, it drops to 150. CarsGuide reported the HiLux SR5 with Toyota's mild-hybrid diesel is claimed at 191 grams per kilometre – already above the 2026 limit, and 41 grams over the 2027 one.

Toyota's John Pappas has called the 2027 targets for light commercial vehicles "really challenging". And Toyota said it "cannot rule out" price increases. That's a warning, not a price list – nobody has announced a 2027 price rise yet. And the first actual penalty bills, for vehicles sold in 2025, aren't due to be determined until February 2028, so car makers still have time to trade credits. But it tells you which way the pressure is pushing.

There's another possibility too. On the 29th of September 2026, the federal Coalition pledged to repeal the NVES if it wins government, calling it a "family car and ute tax". So the rules themselves could change. The point is simple: the conditions creating today's deals are unusual, and they might not be here in a year.
