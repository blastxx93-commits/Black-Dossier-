And then there's the ute that's really struggling: the Kia Tasman.

When Kia launched the Tasman in 2025, it was the most important new ute in years – Kia's first ever, designed with Australia in mind. Kia forecast 20,000 sales in its first calendar year, and seven to ten per cent of the segment. It also expected half of those sales to go to fleets. Instead, Kia says the split is roughly one quarter fleet and three quarters private buyers – so the big company orders it was counting on haven't arrived.

The reality? From launch to the end of September 2026, Kia sold 7,898 Tasmans in total. In September, it found 355 buyers – down 56 per cent on the same month last year.

So Kia went hard on price. In May 2026, it cut the Tasman by up to $15,000. The top X-Pro dropped to $64,990 drive-away. The X-Line came down to $59,990, and the dual-cab S 4x4 to $49,990.

Kia's own executives have been remarkably honest. Then-boss Damien Meredith said the exterior is "very polarising". He admitted: "We've fallen short. We know the facts." But he also said, "once you're inside it, it's fantastic." Meredith also pointed out that the Tasman was planned six years ago, and the competition since then has become, in his words, "quite dramatic". And at the end of October, Kia's new chief executive, Dennis Piccoli, said Kia can do a "vastly better job" selling the Tasman, adding: "We'd like to sell more cars. It's as simple as that."
