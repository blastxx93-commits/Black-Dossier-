Now the scoreboard. Here's how the rest of the ute market performed in September 2026, according to VFACTS data published through CarExpert's sales database.

The losers first. The Mitsubishi Triton: 1,202 sales, down 30.6 per cent. The GWM Cannon: 852, down 31.6. The Mazda BT-50: 843, down 22.5. The Nissan Navara: 338, down almost 35. The Volkswagen Amarok: 244, down almost 43. The KGM Musso: 85, down 38. And the JAC T9 Chinese ute: 58, down 47 per cent. Even the Isuzu D-Max, one of the most trusted names in the bush, was down six per cent. Isuzu is in a particularly tough spot, because its whole local range is diesel. In 2026, it cancelled plans to bring the electric D-Max to Australia, with Isuzu's Rod Caldwell saying simply: "Customers don't want it." 

Now the winners. The BYD Shark 6 plug-in hybrid: 1,492 sales, up 25 per cent, and more than 13,500 for the year. LDV's cheaper T60: up almost 69 per cent. LDV's new Terron 9: up 195 per cent. The Shark 6 is a turnaround story of its own. Sales stalled in early 2026 at around 1,200 a month, after plug-in hybrids lost their fringe benefits tax exemption. BYD's chief operating officer in Australia, Stephen Collins, admitted at the time: "I reckon we can do better." By September, Shark sales were back up twenty-five per cent on a year earlier. And big American pick-up trucks – the Ram, Silverado, F-150 and Tundra – were up 13 per cent in September and almost 12 per cent for the year.

So the ute market isn't just shrinking. It's splitting. Buyers are going either cheaper, electrified, or much bigger – and the traditional mid-range diesel dual-cab is getting squeezed from all sides.
