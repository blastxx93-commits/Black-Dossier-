BYD, meanwhile, is turning up the pressure. From the 1st of October to the 21st of December 2026, BYD is offering $4,000 cashback across much of its range – including the Shark 6. CarsGuide listed the Shark 6 cab-chassis Dynamic at $51,990 before on-road costs with the cashback applied, the Premium at $53,900 and the Performance at $62,900. Cars have to be delivered by the 31st of December.

And at the top end of the market, the fight is just as fierce. In May 2026, Mitsubishi priced its new Triton Raider at $74,990 drive-away. CarExpert worked out that a Ford Ranger Tremor came to about $82,404 drive-away for a private buyer in Sydney, and a HiLux Rugged X to $78,117. That's several thousand dollars of difference between three utes that do very similar jobs.

GWM has played the same game. In 2025 it cut its Cannon Alpha plug-in hybrid by up to $7,100, to $57,490 drive-away. Every brand is chasing the same shrinking pool of buyers – and the easiest weapon is price.

And there's more coming. The JAC Hunter plug-in hybrid has been targeting less than fifty thousand dollars drive-away. Chery has a diesel-electric ute due before the end of 2026. GWM plans a plug-in hybrid version of its smaller Cannon. And Nissan's next Navara is set to use Mitsubishi's plug-in hybrid technology. More choice means more competition – and more pressure on prices.
