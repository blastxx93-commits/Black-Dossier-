Now Ford.

Ford's Ranger has been Australia's best-selling vehicle in 2024 and 2025, and it's leading again in 2026. But even the champion has been cutting prices.

In April 2026, Ford slashed the price of the Ranger plug-in hybrid – now simply called the Ranger Hybrid. The XLT Hybrid dropped to $62,000 drive-away. It had been listed at $71,990 before on-road costs, so Ford described that as over ten thousand dollars less. The Sport was $66,000 drive-away, the Wildtrak $70,000, and the Stormtrak $73,000.

At the same time, Ford handed out a $4,000 fuel card on 2026-model Rangers and entry-level Everests, timed with the federal government's cut to fuel excise. Ford's Australian boss, Fadi Mawal, said the offers were about helping customers through "short-term pain at the pump". Farmers registered as primary producers got an extra thousand dollars off any Ranger, including the hybrid and the big Super Duty.

And buyers responded. After the Wildtrak hybrid dropped to $70,000 drive-away, Ford said dealer stock was soon "all but exhausted". As a Ford spokesperson put it: "Australian buyers are doing the maths." That's the key lesson of this whole price war. Cut the price far enough, and buyers come back – even in a falling market.

Here's the interesting part. Ford is discounting from a position of strength. The Ranger is still growing. Ford isn't panicking about the Ranger itself – it's making sure that when Toyota finally swings back, there's no opening.
