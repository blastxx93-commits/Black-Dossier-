Number ten: the fuel card war.

Some brands don't want to cut the price, because that hurts resale values. So instead, they hand you a fuel card.

Chery is offering a two-thousand-dollar fuel card on the Tiggo 7, with the Tiggo 7 Urban at $29,990 drive-away, and a fifteen-hundred-dollar fuel card on the Tiggo 4, which starts at $23,990 drive-away. The Chery E5 electric SUV gets two thousand dollars cashback instead. Those offers run until the 21st of December, according to the deals tracker CarSorted.

Mitsubishi is doing the same thing. There's a three-thousand-dollar fuel card on the Triton ute, and a two-thousand-dollar fuel card on the Outlander LS Black Edition, which is listed at $47,990 drive-away until the end of November.

Now, here's the catch with fuel cards. A two-thousand-dollar fuel card is not the same as two thousand dollars off the price. You can't spend it on anything else, it doesn't reduce your loan, and it doesn't reduce the stamp duty. If a dealer offers you a fuel card, it's always worth asking: could I have that as a discount instead? Sometimes the answer is yes.

And remember the context. Mitsubishi's sales halved in October. When a brand's sales fall that far, there's usually more room to negotiate than the advertised offer.
