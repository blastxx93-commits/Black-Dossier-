First, why is this happening now?

Australians bought 107,493 new vehicles in October 2026, a record for the month. So people are buying. But they're buying very different cars from even a year ago. Electric car sales were up 237 per cent on October last year, BYD sold more than ten thousand cars in a single month, and cars built in China were up 104 per cent.

That means a lot of brands are sitting on stock they can't move – especially utes, big SUVs and older models. Mitsubishi sales were down 50 per cent in October. Renault was down 58 per cent. Hyundai was down almost 29. Even Ford, the maker of Australia's favourite ute, was down 3 per cent.

And there's a second pressure. It's the end of the year. Dealers have annual targets, factories want to clear 2025 and 2026-built cars before the new plates arrive, and Black Friday falls on the 27th of November. So brands are throwing everything at buyers – price cuts, cashback, fuel cards, deposit contributions, gift cards and free servicing.

A quick note before we start. These deals are current as of mid-November 2026. Most of them end on the 30th of November, the 21st of December or the 31st of December. Drive-away prices can change by state, and some deals are only for private buyers. So always get the final price in writing from the dealer.
