So how do you get the best out of these deals?

First, read the fine print. Look for words like "private buyers only", "selected stock", "participating dealers", "not combinable with other offers" and "must be delivered by". Most of these deals end on the 30th of November, the 21st of December or the 31st of December.

Second, always ask for a final drive-away price in writing, for your postcode. Advertised prices can change by state.

Third, prefer a real discount over a fuel card or a gift card. A discount reduces what you borrow and the stamp duty.

Fourth, if a deal depends on finance, compare the interest rate and the total cost of the loan, not just the bonus.

Fifth, ask about demonstrators. Several of these deals, like Suzuki's, include demo stock. A demonstrator with a few thousand kilometres can take another chunk off the price, and it's usually still covered by most of the new-car warranty. Just check the build date, the date the warranty started, and the service history, and make sure the price reflects any wear.

Sixth, timing. The last week of a month, and especially the last weeks of December, is when dealers are most desperate to hit their targets. If a deal ends on the 30th of November, don't be surprised if a new one starts on the 1st of December. But don't wait too long on limited stock – once the cheap cars are gone, they're gone.

And finally, think about resale. The biggest discounts are usually on the cars that aren't selling. That's great for the price you pay today, but it can mean faster depreciation later.
