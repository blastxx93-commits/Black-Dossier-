Number eight: Genesis.

Hyundai's luxury brand has some genuinely impressive cars, but the numbers are brutal. Genesis sold just 66 cars in total across Australia last month – down 54.5 per cent. The GV80 wagon moved two. The GV70, its best seller, managed 46.

The discounts tell the story. In July, Genesis extended end-of-financial-year deals with up to $7,000 off the GV70, and up to $10,000 off the GV80. That's serious money to take off a luxury SUV – and it still isn't moving them in big numbers. Luxury buyers are notoriously loyal to badges they already know, like Mercedes-Benz, BMW and Lexus. Breaking into that club takes years, and Genesis is learning that the hard way.

Number seven: the BMW iX.

BMW's large electric SUV used to be a status symbol. Last month, it found only seven buyers, down almost 88 per cent. Part of the reason is BMW itself. Its new iX3 arrived this year with a much sharper price, and in April BMW cut up to $8,900 from the smaller iX1 and iX2. When a brand's own newer, cheaper electric SUVs look like better value, the old flagship gets squeezed.

That's the second big lesson. Sometimes a car's worst enemy isn't a rival brand. It's the next model in its own showroom.
