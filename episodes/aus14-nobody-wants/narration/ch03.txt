Number ten: the Renault Koleos.

The Koleos is a mid-size family SUV, the kind of car Australians buy by the tens of thousands. Toyota sold more than 4,600 RAV4s in September alone. Renault found homes for eighteen Koleos. That's down 70.5 per cent.

And it's not because it's expensive. The Koleos has been advertised from $36,990 drive-away – cheap for a mid-size SUV in 2026. Even at that price, buyers are walking past it. This generation has been around for years, and back in 2025 Renault Australia's Glen Sealey said a "different vehicle" may replace it, "maybe in 2026". Nothing has been confirmed, and the market has clearly moved on.

That's the first big lesson of this list. A low price can't save a car that feels old. Buyers in 2026 are walking into showrooms expecting hybrid or electric options, big screens and the latest safety tech. If a car doesn't tick those boxes, they'll pay a few thousand more for something newer.

Number nine: the Mazda CX-70.

Mazda's big two-row SUV was supposed to be a premium flagship. Instead, it's become one of the slowest-selling cars in its class. Sixteen sales in September. Just 182 for the whole year so far. To put that in perspective, Mazda sold more than 3,500 of the smaller CX-60 over the same period.

In July 2026, Mazda slashed CX-70 prices by between $3,700 and $8,051 depending on the variant. It didn't fix the problem. And in late October, Mazda confirmed the CX-70 is ending production for the United States, its biggest market. Mazda Australia says the car will continue here, and that it "still has a small but loyal fanbase". But unnamed sources told CarsGuide it will leave the local market soon. Mazda's own global report has called Australia a "tough environment".
